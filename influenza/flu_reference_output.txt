A1  THE EXACT UPPER BOUND ON ANTIVIRAL BENEFIT  (the central result)
==============================================================================
  An antiviral cannot subtract viral load that has already happened.
  So for a drug started at t_rx the reduction in viral AUC is bounded
  above by the RESIDUAL AUC

        R(t_rx) = integral from t_rx to infinity of (log10 V - LOD)+

  which is a property of the UNTREATED trajectory alone -- it contains
  no drug, no potency, no mechanism.  Everything a molecule can ever
  achieve at that time sits underneath it.

  symptom onset at 24.2 h p.i.;  untreated total AUC 19.00 log10.d

  h post-onset  h p.i.   T left     R(t)  R/total  achieved  efficiency
             0    24.2    99.9%    18.61    97.9%     16.57       89.1%
            12    36.2    85.7%    16.99    89.4%     10.19       60.0%
            24    48.2     0.1%    14.27    75.1%      9.37       65.6%
            36    60.2     1.3%    11.26    59.2%      8.68       77.0%
            48    72.2     3.5%     8.65    45.5%      7.67       88.7%
            72    96.2     7.1%     4.65    24.5%      4.47       96.1%
            96   120.2    13.2%     1.96    10.3%      1.90       97.0%

  At the moment the patient first feels ill, 98% of the viral
  AUC is still in the future.  Twenty-four hours later -- the median
  enrolment time in CAPSTONE-1 -- it is 75%; at 48 h, the edge of
  the licensed window, 45%.  The 48-hour rule is not a statement
  about pharmacology.  It is the interval over which the quantity a
  drug could act on falls by roughly an order of magnitude, and it
  would still be there if the drug were infinitely potent.

  The efficiency column answers a different question: given the bound,
  how much of it does a real molecule actually take?  It is NOT flat.
  It is worst just before the viral peak and best long after it,
  because the cohort of cells already infected when the drug arrives
  goes on producing for a full cell lifetime no matter what the drug
  does to the cells it has not reached yet.  Early dosing therefore
  wins on the bound and loses on the efficiency, and the bound wins:
  the largest absolute benefit is still at the earliest dose time.

==============================================================================
  oseltamivir carboxylate, ELF
    Cmax                             420.0 ng/mL   (reported plasma Cmax ~350 ng/mL)
    calibrated in-vivo EC50            3.0 ng/mL
    peak fractional release block     98.8 %
    trough fractional block (48-120 h)   93.5 %

  baloxavir acid
    total Cmax                        83.8 ng/mL   (reported 68.9 ng/mL for 40 mg)
    free Cmax (fu = 0.07)              5.65 ng/mL = 11.7 nM
    in-vitro EC50                    0.677 ng/mL = 1.4 nM
    calibrated in-vivo EC50          0.052 ng/mL = 0.11 nM
    ratio in-vivo / in-vitro          0.08 x
    Hill slope (calibrated)            2.0
    peak fractional block          99.9815 %
    residual production fraction  1.85e-04

  DISCREPANCY 1.  The CAPSTONE-1 day-2 titre fall CANNOT be produced by a
  Michaelis (Hill = 1) concentration-response at any Emax whatsoever.
  With h = 1 the residual production fraction is bounded below by
  EC50/(C + EC50), so at the achieved free concentration the fall
  saturates near -3.3 log10 in 24 h however large Emax is made -- the
  published value is -4.8.  Only a steeper-than-Michaelis slope
  reaches it; this model uses h = 2.0, calibrated, and reports the
  requirement rather than hiding it inside Emax.  Note what that means:
  the trial datum is evidence about the SHAPE of the concentration-
  response, not only about the potency, and a QSP model fitted on Emax
  alone would have absorbed a shape error into a potency parameter.

  Alongside it, the calibrated in-vivo EC50 is 13x LOWER than the
  in-vitro EC50 on a free-drug basis.  Either free plasma drug
  understates what reaches the intracellular compartment where the PA
  endonuclease sits, or the nasal-wash titre falls faster than
  infectious-centre biology alone because non-infectious particle
  output is silenced too.  The model cannot choose between them.

A4  TIMING AND POTENCY ARE NOT THE SAME KIND OF VARIABLE
==============================================================================
  A1 established the bound: a drug started at t_rx can remove at most
  R(t_rx), the untreated residual AUC.  That gives the two axes
  different characters, and this analysis measures both HEADROOMS
  rather than asserting which is larger:

    potency headroom  =  R(t_rx) - achieved(t_rx, Emax)
        how much of the bound a more potent drug could still take
    timing headroom   =  R(t_earlier) - R(t_rx)
        how much the bound itself moves if you dose earlier

    potency headroom (Emax -> 1)        +0.09 log.d
    timing headroom  (dose at onset)    +4.34 log.d

  The timing headroom is the larger of the two by a wide margin, and
  it is the only one a better molecule cannot buy.  Making the drug
  perfect adds almost nothing, because the drug is already taking
  nearly all of a bound that delay has already shrunk.  This is the
  quantitative content of 'treat early': not that late treatment is
  weak, but that late treatment is being asked to act on a quantity
  that has mostly already been spent.

  Note the non-monotone TTAS in the timing sweep: dosing before the
  cytokine surge has begun can abort the illness outright, so the
  endpoint is not merely shortened, it is never entered.

A5  OPERATOR DECOMPOSITION -- which term of the loop does each drug touch
==============================================================================
  Each row switches ON exactly one operator at 95% efficacy from t_rx,
  with no drug PK, so the operators can be compared on equal terms.

  operator                           TTAS h   shed h   d24 log  AUC log.d  epi lost
  PRODUCTION / RELEASE  (p)            66.2     88.5     -2.63      13.89     85.1%
  TRANSCRIPTION        (E->I)          73.5    114.5     -1.52      18.23     85.7%
  ENTRY                (beta)          78.2    117.5     -1.34      18.92     84.1%
  VIRION CLEARANCE     (c)             65.8     88.0     -2.70      13.24     84.2%
  INFECTED-CELL DEATH  (delta)         32.2     29.0     -4.16       9.56     72.4%
  TARGET PROTECTION    (T->R)          78.5    117.5     -1.33      18.94     84.7%
  (none: placebo)                      78.7    118.0     -1.32      19.00     85.6%

  The signatures differ in KIND, not only in size.  Read the columns
  against each other rather than down:
   - ENTRY and TARGET PROTECTION barely move the 24-hour fall, because
     the cells already infected when the operator switches on keep
     producing; these operators only stop the NEXT generation.
   - PRODUCTION/RELEASE and VIRION CLEARANCE both cut the titre
     promptly and by almost the same amount -- unsurprising, since at
     quasi-steady state the titre is p*I/c and they are the numerator
     and denominator of the same ratio.
   - INFECTED-CELL DEATH is the strongest single operator in the model,
     because it is the only one that removes the source rather than
     throttling it.  No licensed influenza antiviral works this way;
     the immune system does, which is why the natural history is so
     much more decisive than any of the drugs acting on it.
   - TRANSCRIPTION (diverting eclipse cells to an abortive fate) is
     WEAKER at the same 95% than production blockade, which is not
     obvious: it only redirects cells that have yet to mature, while
     production blockade also silences every cell that already has.
     Baloxavir beats the neuraminidase inhibitors in the ledger
     despite acting at the weaker point, purely on achieved potency.

  This is why 'antiviral efficacy' is not a single number: two drugs
  with the same effect on viral AUC can have quite different effects
  on the 24-hour fall, on shedding duration, and on epithelial loss.

  Target cells still available at each dose time (untreated run):
           0 h post-onset:  T =  99.95 % of T0
          12 h post-onset:  T =  85.73 % of T0
          24 h post-onset:  T =   0.10 % of T0
          36 h post-onset:  T =   1.27 % of T0
          48 h post-onset:  T =   3.54 % of T0
          72 h post-onset:  T =   7.10 % of T0

  The maximum sits at dose time 0 h post-onset, Emax = 0.9, giving a mutant peak of 6.19 log10.

  Two things fall out that were not put in:

  (1) ALONG THE POTENCY AXIS the mutant does not simply fall as the
      drug improves.  At Emax = 0 it loses to a fitter wild type; as
      the drug improves it stops losing, because its competitor is
      being removed and the target cells the wild type would have
      taken are handed to it instead.  Selection for resistance is a
      by-product of the drug WORKING, not of it failing.

  (2) ALONG THE TIMING AXIS there is a hard prerequisite that is easy
      to miss: competitive release needs something to release.  Dosing
      late selects far less -- not because the drug is weaker, but
      because the wild type has already consumed the epithelium and
      there is no spared field for the mutant to grow in.  Resistance
      emergence and clinical benefit therefore share a prerequisite,
      and the same policy -- treat earlier -- buys more of both.

  DISCREPANCY 4.  This is a deterministic model, so a mutant lineage at
  10^-4 of the population is 'present' in every simulated patient.
  Real I38T emergence is a stochastic establishment event in ~10% of
  treated adults.  The peaks above are expectations over an ensemble
  that never happens to any single patient, and should be read as a
  selection pressure, not as a predicted viral load.

A7  WHO SELECTS I38T -- AND WHERE THIS MODEL GETS IT WRONG
==============================================================================
  CAPSTONE-1 reported PA/I38T/M/F in 9.7% of baloxavir-treated adults
  (Hayden 2018, PMID 30184455); paediatric trials have reported higher
  frequencies (miniSTONE-2, Baker 2020, PMID 32516282).  The model is
  given NO age term.  The only differences below are prior immunity and the
  size of the epithelial target pool.  Every arm is dosed 12 h after
  its own symptom onset, i.e. while a field still exists to release.

  host                        shed h  mut peak  mut>LOD  mut AUC  T at dose
  adult, prior exposure        208.2      4.28    8.68d    17.90      91.8%
  adult, naive                 143.5      2.90    5.98d     8.44      85.7%
  child (naive, larger T)       78.2      1.86    3.16d     3.19      58.3%
  immunocompromised            246.2      5.87   10.26d    29.33      86.2%

  Read the mutant AUC column rather than the peak: what a resistant
  lineage needs in order to be sampled and reported is TIME above the
  assay floor, not a high maximum.

  DISCREPANCY 5 -- and it is the model's clearest failure.  The ordering
  comes out BACKWARDS.  The adult with pre-existing immunity selects
  the most mutant of the three immunocompetent hosts and the child the
  least, which is the reverse of the paediatric-versus-adult signal.

  The mechanism producing the reversal is visible in the last column,
  and it is not a coding accident: competitive release is governed
  almost entirely by T(t_rx), the field still standing when the drug
  arrives, and ANYTHING that slows the wild type leaves more of it.
  Pre-existing CD8 memory and mucosal IgA slow the wild type.  So in
  this structure prior immunity increases the field, and therefore
  increases the release.  The child's larger epithelium makes the wild
  type faster, so by 12 h post-onset the child has consumed more of its
  own field and has less left to hand over.

  Two honest readings, and the model cannot choose:
   - the structure is right and the clinical comparison is confounded
     (children are brought to care earlier in their illness, are dosed
     by weight band, and shed longer, all of which push the other way);
   - or target-cell availability is NOT the dominant control on I38T
     emergence, and a well-mixed epithelium is the wrong idealisation
     for a mucosa where the two strains are spatially separated.

  What the model does get right is the immunocompromised host, whose
  mutant AUC is the largest by a wide margin -- consistent with the
  prolonged shedding and repeated resistance emergence reported in that
  population.  There, both mechanisms point the same way.

A8  WHAT THE SYMPTOM ENDPOINT REQUIRES OF THE MODEL
==============================================================================
  The virological endpoints of CAPSTONE-1 are reproduced by viral
  dynamics alone.  The SYMPTOM endpoint is not, and the reason is
  structural rather than numerical.

  If the symptom score is driven purely by the cytokine state (WVIR = 0),
  its size and timing are settled by the interferon peak, which happens
  BEFORE any drug given at the median enrolment time can act.  Under
  that structure no antiviral, at any potency, shortens the illness.
  WVIR is the fraction of the symptom drive that instead tracks the
  instantaneous titre.  Sweeping it asks the trial a question:
  how much of how a patient feels has to be same-day virus?

    WVIR   onset  placebo    oselt    balox    oselt    balox
          h p.i.   TTAS h   TTAS h   TTAS h  benefit  benefit
    0.00    42.8     55.5     55.5     54.8      0.0      0.8
    0.20    33.2     66.2     62.2     57.5      4.0      8.8
    0.40    27.0     74.2     64.5     53.2      9.8     21.0
    0.60    24.2     78.7     63.5     44.0     15.2     34.7
    0.80    23.0     82.5     59.7     36.0     22.8     46.5
    1.00    22.0     86.2     53.5     32.0     32.7     54.2

  CAPSTONE-1 measured a 26.5 h benefit for both drugs.  At WVIR = 0 the
  model gives essentially zero for either.  The published symptom
  endpoint therefore cannot be explained by a cytokine-only symptom
  model, and the value this model carries (WVIR = 0.60) is the price of
  matching it.  That is a falsifiable claim about influenza symptoms,
  not a fitting convenience: it says a substantial part of how a
  patient feels tracks today's replication rather than the size of the
  cytokine surge that has already passed.

  DISCREPANCY 3.  Even at the calibrated WVIR the model splits the two
  drugs, giving baloxavir a larger symptom benefit than oseltamivir,
  whereas CAPSTONE-1 found them indistinguishable (53.7 vs 53.8 h)
  despite a 2-log difference in day-2 titre.  A model in which symptoms
  track virus at all cannot reproduce two arms that differ 100-fold in
  virus and not at all in symptoms.  Either the symptom-virus coupling
  saturates well below the titres these drugs separate, or the
  patient-reported endpoint is not resolving what the assay is.

A9  CORTICOSTEROIDS: SHORT-TERM COMFORT PAID FOR IN VIRAL AUC
==============================================================================
  arm                                          AUC   shed h  peak sym   TTAS h   bact
  oseltamivir alone                          12.96     85.2      12.1     63.5   5.86
  oseltamivir + dexamethasone 6 mg x5d       13.07     85.2      12.0     60.2   5.66
  dexamethasone alone                        19.33    122.0      12.3     76.5   5.69

  The direction is right and the size is small, and both are worth
  saying.  Dexamethasone lowers the symptom score and shortens time to
  alleviation -- it acts directly on the cytokine arm that the score
  reads -- while raising viral AUC and lengthening shedding, because
  the same suppression removes interferon-driven target protection and
  CD8 killing.  That is the shape of the observational mortality signal
  (Lansbury 2019 Cochrane, PMID 30798570) recovered from mechanism
  alone, with no mortality term anywhere in the model.

  But the magnitude is a caveat on the model, not a reassurance about
  the drug.  In THIS parameterisation neither interferon-driven target
  protection nor CD8 killing is rate-limiting for clearance -- target
  exhaustion and antibody are -- so suppressing them costs little.  A
  model calibrated on a host in which the CD8 response IS rate-limiting
  (the elderly, the immunosuppressed) would show a much larger penalty,
  and those are exactly the patients the observational signal comes
  from.  The small effect here should be read as a property of the
  calibration, not as evidence that steroids are nearly harmless.

A11  THE LOWER TRACT AND THE BACTERIAL SEQUEL
==============================================================================
  Secondary bacterial pneumonia is modelled as the product of two
  influenza-created conditions: exposed adhesion sites on denuded
  epithelium, and interferon-mediated suppression of antibacterial
  defence (Shahangian 2009 J Clin Invest, PMID 19487810).

  arm                                 LRT peak  LRT damage  bact peak  >threshold
  placebo                                 3.87        0.2%       5.90          no
  oseltamivir 24 h post-onset             3.72        0.1%       5.86          no
  baloxavir 24 h post-onset               3.71        0.1%       5.76          no
  baloxavir at onset                     -0.29        0.0%       2.02          no
  baloxavir 72 h post-onset               3.87        0.2%       5.90          no
  placebo, 5x LRT seeding                 4.56        0.8%       5.93          no
  placebo, blunted interferon             3.88        0.2%       5.62          no

  Two things worth reading off this table rather than assuming.

  First, in the uncomplicated adult the bacterial burden climbs about
  four logs and still does not reach the superinfection threshold in
  ANY antiviral arm.  Timing barely moves it.  The only arm that
  changes the bacterial endpoint is the one that aborts the infection
  outright -- which is the honest version of 'antivirals prevent
  secondary pneumonia': in this model they do it by preventing the
  influenza, not by softening it.

  Second -- and this is the limitation, not a result -- NOTHING in the
  table crosses the threshold, including the two deliberately worsened
  arms.  The threshold itself (6.0 log10 CFU) has no independent
  calibration; it is a stipulation.  So this module can ORDER arms by
  bacterial risk, which is what the ordering above is for, and it
  cannot say who develops pneumonia.  Any absolute risk read off it
  would be reading the stipulation back out.
