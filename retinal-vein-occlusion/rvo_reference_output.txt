      Non-ischaemic CRVO crosses below its critical pressure as collaterals
      mature; ischaemic CRVO never does.  That single crossing time, not the
      choice of anti-VEGF agent, is what separates the two clinical courses.

      Same eyes under the best REAL regimen (aflibercept T&E):
        non-ischaemic CRVO  CST m36 = 257 um (perfect blockade 250 um)
        ischaemic CRVO      CST m36 = 363 um (perfect blockade 286 um)
        residual attributable to imperfect blockade: 7 um (non-ischaemic)
        residual attributable to the PRESSURE ARM: 36 um (ischaemic)

      THE PRESSURE ARM AMPLIFIES WHATEVER PERMEABILITY REMAINS.
      Jv = Lp * bracket, so an identical residual elevation of Lp delivers
      more fluid when the bracket is larger.  Cost of the SAME imperfect
      blockade (real aflibercept T&E minus infinite blockade, month 36):
        non-ischaemic (bracket small)     7.3 um
        ischaemic     (bracket large)    77.2 um   = 10.6x
      This is why switching agent fails in a high-pressure eye: the
      pharmacological shortfall being amplified is already small.
