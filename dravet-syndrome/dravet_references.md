# Dravet Syndrome QSP Model — References

All entries were resolved through the NCBI E-utilities API rather than written
from memory, and every PMID below links to the record whose title is quoted.
This matters: an earlier draft of this model cited Chiron 2000 as PMID 11095824,
which is a different paper entirely. The correct STICLO record is 11089822.

**154 references**, grouped by the role they play in the model.
Entries marked **Calibration anchor** are the published quantities the model is
fitted to; there are eight such parameters and they are listed in
[`dravet_mrgsolve_model.R`](dravet_mrgsolve_model.R). Entries marked
**Out-of-sample check** or **Independent confirmation** were *not* fitted.

  - **The parameter the central conclusion rests on.** In 50 patients on low-dose add-on clobazam, seizure frequency fell significantly only in CYP2C19 poor metabolisers, and median norclobazam was 1103 ng/mL in those with ≥90% seizure reduction versus 341 and 570 ng/mL in the less-controlled groups.   - **Independent confirmation of a model prediction.** In 11 Japanese Dravet patients, stiripentol response showed no significant relationship to CYP2C19 genotype; 3 of 8 had no benefit despite a rise in norclobazam, and 1 of 3 benefited despite norclobazam *falling*. The authors concluded this 'suggests a significant anti-convulsant action of STP' in its own right — which is what the decomposition in this model concludes quantitatively.
- Saruwatari J et al. *Effects of CYP2C19 and P450 oxidoreductase polymorphisms on the population pharmacokinetics of clobazam and N-desmethylclobazam in japanese patients with epilepsy.* Ther Drug Monit 2014. [PMID 24345815](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24345815/)

## Notes on evidence quality

Three limitations in this evidence base shape what the model can and cannot
claim, and they are worth stating plainly:

1. **There is no clobazam-free stiripentol trial.** STICLO was run entirely on
   a valproate + clobazam background. The clobazam-free stiripentol column in
   the model output is therefore an extrapolation, not a reproduction of data.
   The nearest real evidence is Kouga 2015, which is 11 patients.
2. **The norclobazam exposure-response comes from a mixed-epilepsy cohort**
   (Hashi 2015), not from Dravet syndrome, and it is retrospective. The model's
   central conclusion depends on transferring that saturation point to Dravet.
   If the true Dravet exposure-response saturates much higher, the conclusion
   weakens.
3. **Placebo responses are not comparable across these trials** — +19.2% in
   Study 1, +26.9% in GWPCARE2, but −7% in STICLO, a 1990s trial of 41
   children with a one-month baseline. All cross-trial comparisons here are
   placebo-adjusted for that reason, which is a partial correction and not a
   complete one.
