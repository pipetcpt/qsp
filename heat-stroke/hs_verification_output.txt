  Reading: at rest the boundary sits at a wet-bulb of 35.7 C -- the
  canonical 35 C survivability limit falls out of the heat-balance
  equation without being put in.  At 900 W it drops to 20.7 C.
  The 35 C limit is the RESTING limit.  Working people lose the fixed
  point many degrees of wet-bulb earlier, which is the empirical
  finding of Vecellio 2022 obtained here from the equation alone.

  ACCLIMATISATION DOES NOT MOVE THIS BOUNDARY.  Mean shift -0.27 C
  (range -0.51 to -0.03), and the sign is not
  consistently favourable.  The reason is visible in the solution: at
  every point on this boundary E_actual == E_max,environment, while
  sweat capacity is 668 W unacclimatised and 1233 W acclimatised.
  Neither is ever reached.  The AIR is the binding constraint, so
  doubling the gland's output buys nothing.

  This is a falsifiable structural claim, not a hedge: heat
  acclimatisation -- and every other sweat-side countermeasure -- must
  act on the TRANSIENT (how fast the core climbs, how much
  cardiovascular reserve is left) rather than on WHICH environments
  are survivable.  Section 8 tests that consequence.

NEHS: elderly+anticholinergic, 43C/45%          374      622      890    0.0075 C/min       268 min

  The interval between crossing 40.0 C -- the diagnostic threshold --
  and reaching 42.0 C IS the warning time a temperature-based
  criterion gives you.  It differs between the two heat strokes by
  a factor of 19 (20 min vs 375 min).
  It is the same threshold on two clocks running at different speeds,
  and that -- not a difference in intrinsic lethality -- is the first
  half of why classic heat stroke kills more often than exertional.

3. REGIME 2 -> the DOSE.  CEM43 = the integral the patient actually
   pays.  Cooling modality is a POTENCY; delay is an EXPOSURE TIME.
==============================================================================
  Sapareto-Dewey: dose rate = R^(43-Tc) equivalent minutes per minute,
  R = 0.25 below 43 C, 0.50 at or above.  Each degree below 43 divides
  the rate by four.  Time and temperature are NOT exchangeable 1:1.

     Tc:   40.0     41.0     42.0     42.5     43.0     43.5     44.0
   rate:    0.016    0.062    0.250    0.500    1.000    1.414    2.000   CEM43-min per min

  A. Total CEM43 paid from collapse at 42.0 C to 38.6 C:
  modality                           delay 0  delay 10  delay 30  delay 60  cool min
  ice-water immersion (2C)              0.90      2.97      6.22      9.52      17.0
  cold-water immersion (14C)            1.12      3.16      6.35      9.60      22.0
  tarp-assisted cooling (10C)           1.35      3.36      6.49      9.69      27.0
  cold shower / dousing (20C)           1.85      3.78      6.81      9.90      40.0
  evaporative + convective              2.32      4.19      7.11     10.09      51.0
  endovascular catheter                 3.18      4.91      7.64     10.42      68.0
  ice packs (neck/axilla/groin)         6.27      7.56      9.58     11.66     136.0
  passive (shade, no cooling)          15.67     15.67     15.67     15.67     364.0

  B. THE EXCHANGE RATE.  Upgrading the cooler buys you a fixed number
     of minutes of delay, and the number is remarkably stable.
     collapse at 41.0 C: upgrading evaporative + convective -> ice-water immersion (2C)
        saves   0.38 CEM43; one minute of delay costs 0.062 CEM43
        => the better cooler is worth   6.0 MINUTES of delay
     collapse at 42.0 C: upgrading evaporative + convective -> ice-water immersion (2C)
        saves   1.42 CEM43; one minute of delay costs 0.250 CEM43
        => the better cooler is worth   5.7 MINUTES of delay
     collapse at 43.0 C: upgrading evaporative + convective -> ice-water immersion (2C)
        saves   4.57 CEM43; one minute of delay costs 1.000 CEM43
        => the better cooler is worth   4.6 MINUTES of delay

     The exchange rate barely moves with peak temperature, but the
     absolute stakes rise exponentially.  This is the quantitative
     content of 'cool first, transport second': modality is a factor
     of a few, delay is unbounded.

4. REGIME 3 -- the commitment switch.  Cooling restores the
   temperature; it does not always restore the patient.
==============================================================================
  effective HMGB1 clearance c_eff = 0.00307/min
  fixed points: OFF = 0.0,  UNSTABLE = 10.91 ng/mL,  ON = 50.3 ng/mL
  saddle-node at H = 21.4 ng/mL: if c_eff exceeds 0.00498/min
  the ON state ceases to exist (requires +0.00192/min of extra clearance).

  A. For each modality, the DELAY at which the switch latches, and
     the CEM43 that delay corresponds to (collapse at 42.0 C).
     The switch is scored at 48 h, not at the end of cooling: the
     temperature is settled within the hour and the switch is not.
  modality                          crit delay   CEM43 paid by then
  ice-water immersion (2C)                44.7                 8.03
  cold-water immersion (14C)              43.4                 7.99
  tarp-assisted cooling (10C)             42.9                 8.05
  cold shower / dousing (20C)             40.5                 8.06
  evaporative + convective                38.3                 8.09
  endovascular catheter                   34.1                 8.09
  ice packs (neck/axilla/groin)           17.2                 8.36

  The committing DOSE is 8.1 +/- 0.1 CEM43 across modalities (spread 0.36),
  while the committing DELAY ranges from 17 to 45 minutes.
  The dose is a property of the patient; the delay you can afford
  is a property of the cooler.  That is the whole argument for
  cooling on the field, expressed as two numbers.

6. WHY ANTIPYRETICS CANNOT WORK -- and what they cost.
==============================================================================
  Fever raises the SET-POINT and the body defends it.  Heat stroke
  leaves the set-point at 37 C and saturates the effectors trying to
  hold it.  An antipyretic lowers a threshold that is no longer the
  binding constraint.  The model makes this a sensitivity, not a claim:

  A 0.5 C set-point shift is applied, and the effect is read off both
  in absolute terms and as a FRACTION of the rate of rise.  The
  fraction is the number that matters.

  condition                            HSI   sweat   SkBF    dTc/dt   d(rate)  as % of rate  dCEM43@90
  compensable (rest, 30C/50%)           0%  0.00L/h   1.96   -0.0011   -0.0009        +82.7%     -0.002
  marginal (400 W, 33C/60%)            91%  0.49L/h   3.52   +0.0006   -0.0000         -1.2%     -0.011
  uncompensable (900 W, 35C/80%)      171%  1.05L/h   4.88   +0.0724   +0.0007         +1.0%     -0.598

  The ABSOLUTE effect of moving the set-point is about the same in
  all three regimes -- roughly a thousandth of a degree per minute.
  What changes by two orders of magnitude is what that is a fraction
  OF.  In a compensable exposure it is several times the entire rate
  of change; in an uncompensable one it is about 1% of it, and the
  sign is not even reliably favourable.

  That is the whole antipyretic argument, and note what it is NOT: it
  is not that antipyretics are weak drugs.  They move the set-point
  exactly as advertised.  They are ineffective here because in heat
  stroke the set-point is not the binding constraint -- the effectors
  are already saturated against it.  Antipyretics work precisely in
  the region where the patient does not have heat stroke.

  And the cost side.  Paracetamol 1 g IV on a heat-injured liver,
  with CYP2E1 induced and glutathione already being spent:
    noPARA     peak ALT     225 U/L | peak AST     263 | nadir GSH  1.00 | end liver function 0.990 | peak Tc 41.93
    PARA1g     peak ALT     229 U/L | peak AST     268 | nadir GSH  0.90 | end liver function 0.990 | peak Tc 41.93
    PARA4g     peak ALT     240 U/L | peak AST     275 | nadir GSH  0.75 | end liver function 0.988 | peak Tc 41.93
    Peak core temperature is unchanged to two decimals; the liver is not.

   the commitment variable, and therefore the only one with a window
   set by the switch rather than by the thermometer.
==============================================================================

  half dose              c_eff=0.00427/min  -> unstable 14.66, ON  33.0 ng/mL
  standard 380 U/kg/d    c_eff=0.00547/min  -> MONOSTABLE (ON state abolished)
  double                 c_eff=0.00707/min  -> MONOSTABLE (ON state abolished)

  Read the two blocks together.  At the standard dose the model makes
  a strong and uncomfortable claim: because +0.0024/min of clearance
  puts c_eff past the saddle-node, the ON state ceases to EXIST, so
  the agent reverses an already-latched switch and there is no time
  window at all.  At half dose the ON state survives at 33 ng/mL and
  a window reappears.  The window is therefore not a pharmacokinetic
  property of the drug -- it is a property of which side of the
  saddle-node the dose puts you on.  That is a falsifiable structural
  prediction and it is the model's most exposed therapeutic claim:
  the sepsis-DIC trial of this agent (SCARLET) was null.
