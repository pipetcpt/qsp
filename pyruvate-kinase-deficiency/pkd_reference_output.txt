2.1  The compensation knee is a closed-form quantity, not a fitted one
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Maximum erythroid amplification            = 64.0 x
  Amplification at basal erythropoietin      = 9.56 x
  => maximum sustainable production increase = 6.70 x
  => critical red cell lifespan L* = 120 / 6.70 = 17.9 d

  Above L* the marrow can replace what is destroyed and haemoglobin is
  FLAT in the genotype; below L* production is capped and Hb falls in
  proportion to lifespan.  The table above shows exactly that: lifespan
  moves smoothly from 34 d to 6 d across the spectrum, while Hb is
  nearly unchanged until the lifespan crosses 18 d and then falls fast.

  Two consequences that are not obvious from the bedside:
   (a) haemoglobin is a SATURATING readout of the lesion above L* and a
       hypersensitive one just below it.  Patients cluster near L*, so
       small changes in red cell lifespan produce large Hb swings -- which
       is the same reason a modest intervention can look dramatic.
   (b) reticulocyte count and bilirubin keep rising monotonically THROUGH
       the knee, so in the compensated range they, not Hb, carry the
       information about severity.

SECTION 3.  THE HAEMOGLOBIN ENDPOINT AND THE OXYGEN IT DOES NOT MEASURE
==============================================================================
  Every registrational trial in this disease scores a haemoglobin rise:
  ACTIVATE required >=1.5 g/dL sustained (PMID 35417638), DRIVE-PK used
  >1.0 g/dL (PMID 31483964).  A pyruvate kinase activator raises
  haemoglobin BY LOWERING 2,3-BPG, and lowering 2,3-BPG shifts the oxygen
  dissociation curve back to the LEFT.  So the drug adds carrier and
  subtracts unloading at the same time.  This section asks whether the
  two cancel, and by how much.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  An untreated patient is already partly compensated by the shunt
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A normal subject at Hb 15.0, P50 26.8 mmHg extracts 0.2486 of the O2
  carried per litre of blood down to a tissue PO2 of 38 mmHg.
  The patient at Hb 8.11, P50 33.6 mmHg extracts 0.3600 -- 44.8% more per
  gram.  His EQUIVALENT haemoglobin (the Hb a normal-P50 subject would
  need to unload the same oxygen) is 11.75 g/dL, not 8.11.
  So of the 6.89 g/dL haemoglobin deficit, the shunt has already paid back
  3.64 g/dL -- 53% of it -- before any treatment.
  This is the quantitative form of a well-known bedside observation: PK-
  deficient patients tolerate haemoglobins that would incapacitate other
  anaemic patients.  It is not stoicism, it is a right-shifted curve.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2  The break-even 2,3-BPG reduction, in closed form
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A drug that delivers dHb g/dL leaves oxygen transport unchanged when
      Hb1 * extract(P50_1) = Hb0 * extract(P50_0),
  i.e. when extract(P50_1)/extract(P50_0) = Hb0/Hb1.  Solving that for
  P50, then inverting P50 = P50ref*(DPG/DPG0)^n for 2,3-BPG, gives the
  LARGEST 2,3-BPG reduction compatible with a net oxygen-transport gain.

       dHb        Hb1       P50*     2,3-BPG*     max fall
       1.0       9.11      31.06        7.608        21.5%
       1.5       9.61      30.05        6.861        29.2%
       2.0      10.11      29.15        6.243        35.6%
       3.0      11.11      27.63        5.278        45.5%

  Read the last column as a falsifiable threshold.  At a +1.5 g/dL
  haemoglobin response -- exactly the ACTIVATE primary endpoint -- the
  drug may lower 2,3-BPG by at most the percentage shown before the
  oxygen-transport gain becomes a loss.  Phase 1 in healthy volunteers
  (PMID 30091852) and the phase 2/3 programme both report 2,3-BPG
  reductions of that order, so the model's claim is that the Hb endpoint
  and the physiological endpoint are close to CANCELLING, and which side
  of the line a given patient lands on depends on his baseline 2,3-BPG.

4.2  Why the haemoglobin response is FAST (median 10 d in DRIVE-PK)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       day       Hb      dHb % of wk-24 effect

       112    10.88    +2.77             99%
       168    10.90    +2.79            100%

  DRIVE-PK reported a median 10 days to the first >1.0 g/dL rise, with a
  range out to 187 days (PMID 31483964).  In an age-structured model that
  is not a puzzle and not a pharmacokinetic fact -- it is arithmetic.  A
  step change in hazard relaxes the red cell pool with a time constant
  equal to the NEW mean lifespan, not to the 120 d of a normal cell.  The
  patient's baseline lifespan here is 8 d, so the pool refills in weeks.

  PREDICTION, and it is a strong one: time-to-response should be
  INVERSELY related to baseline red cell lifespan.  The sicker the
  patient (the shorter the lifespan), the FASTER the haemoglobin moves.
  The 187-day outliers should be the mildest patients.  This is testable
  in the existing DRIVE-PK/ACTIVATE datasets without new samples.

  24-week trial and should appear in the long-term extension -- which is
  where the ACTIVATE extension found responses sustained and deepening to
  week 96 (PMID 38330179).

5.2  Splenectomy: a two-line identity the data can refute
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  If the splenic contribution is a hazard on reticulocytes with reversible
  pooling, then in STEADY STATE the circulating reticulocyte pool is
      R = influx / (k_exit + k_in*k_kill/(k_out+k_kill))
  Reversible holding cancels out of R entirely -- cells that are released
  come back.  Only the KILLING term suppresses R.  Writing q for the
  fraction of marrow output killed in the spleen,
      k_in*k_kill/(k_out+k_kill) = k_exit * q/(1-q)
  so removing the spleen raises R by q/(1-q) -- and raises haemoglobin by
  the same q/(1-q), because the same q is the production that was being
  wasted.  THE TWO FRACTIONAL CHANGES MUST BE EQUAL.

  phenotype                           dHb    dHb %   dRet %  seqfrac     L->L
  very mild / compensated           -0.77     -4.9   +104.9     0.61   34-> 34
  mild                              +0.36     +2.6    -41.7     0.66   19-> 21
  moderate                          +2.09    +18.2     -8.9     0.75   12-> 14
  severe, not transfused            +2.61    +32.2     +2.3     0.78    8-> 11
  transfusion dependent             +2.28    +41.8    +13.1     0.79    6->  9

  model median haemoglobin gain +2.09 g/dL, median reticulocyte change +2.3%
  observed median haemoglobin gain +1.6 g/dL (Grace 2018, PMID 29549173)

  This is the model's clearest NEGATIVE result.  PK deficiency is widely
  described as showing a paradoxical RISE in reticulocyte count after
  splenectomy, attributed to splenic reticulocyte sequestration (Nathan
  1968, PMID 5634483).  The model says a sequestration-plus-killing
  mechanism cannot deliver a large reticulocyte rise together with a small
  haemoglobin rise, because both are the SAME q.  Sixteen combinations of
  the two hazard gains were searched before this was recognised as
  structural rather than a calibration failure.  Either the reported
  reticulocyte rise is of the same modest size as the haemoglobin rise
  (~20%, which the model produces), or its cause is NOT splenic
  reticulocyte destruction -- the remaining candidates being the
  maturation-factor inflation of the count and persisting marrow drive.
  Both are measurable, and they make opposite predictions for the
  ABSOLUTE reticulocyte count.

SECTION 7.  WHAT WENT WRONG WHILE BUILDING THIS, AND WHAT IS STILL WRONG
==============================================================================

  Thirteen defects were found by running the equations.  Each is marked at its
  fix site in this file and in the mrgsolve model.  They are listed because the
  ones that mattered were not coding slips -- they were places where a
  physiologically plausible equation gave a quantitatively impossible answer.

   #1  2,3-BPG barely moved.  A bare phosphoglycerate-kinase-equilibrium form
       makes 2,3-BPG proportional to [3-PG]x(ATP/ADP); a PK lesion raises the
       first and lowers the second and they cancel, predicting +9% against an
       observed 2-3 fold.  Fixed by adding the 3-PG inhibition of the 2,3-BPG
       PHOSPHATASE, so the block shuts the shunt's exit as well as feeding it.
   #2  Solving for ATP and 2,3-BPG by simultaneous damped iteration DIVERGED,
       because the FBP -> PKR activation limb is positive feedback.  Fixed by
       nesting: the inner map in 2,3-BPG is monotone and contracts; the outer
       ATP balance is bracketed by a scan.
   #3  The shunt-flux calibration omitted the inhibition factor that the rate
       law uses, so the calibrated normal state was not a steady state.
   #4  The comment claiming the deoxyhaemoglobin-binding limb amplifies flux was
       wrong: free 2,3-BPG is EXACTLY invariant to haemoglobin saturation in this
       model.  The limb shifts total 2,3-BPG by sequestration only.  Kept,
       because that invariance is what makes the lookup tables 2-D instead of 3-D.
   #5  Erythroid iron uptake as an independent saturable process does not
       conserve iron; the healthy subject loaded to 175 mg Fe/g dw.
   #6  A units error made the liver a 0.42 mg iron sink instead of 420 mg.
   #7  Michaelis-Menten bilirubin conjugation calibrated at the normal load
       saturates at ~3x normal haemolysis; a moderately affected patient was
       assigned a bilirubin of 23000 mg/dL.
   #8  Sixteen combinations of the two haemolysis gains, with one shared ATP
       threshold, all gave the WRONG SIGN for the reticulocyte response to
       splenectomy.
   #9  Making splenic reticulocyte destruction strong enough to matter forces
       splenectomy to predict +7 to +12 g/dL instead of +1.6.  This turned out
       to be structural, not a calibration failure -- see Section 5.2.
  #10  The adenylate dilution term divides by cohort cell number and overflowed
       when a cohort was nearly annihilated.
  #11  tau_PK = 25 d made the genotype-to-haemoglobin map a step function.
  #12  Reticulocyte capture was summed over two cohorts and debited to one; once
       the debited cohort hit its floor the model CREATED cells and reported
       haemoglobins of 19-54 g/dL.  Mass balance in an age-structured model is
       per-cohort, not aggregate.
  #13  Explicit RK4 at the burn-in step of 0.25-0.30 d violates the stability
       limit (0.093 d) for the bilirubin and drug-partitioning states.  The
       bilirubin state oscillated into its non-negativity floor and reported
       0.0 mg/dL in a haemolysing patient, while the haemoglobin and
       reticulocyte columns of the step-convergence table still agreed to 1e-5.
       A convergence check on slow outputs alone does not detect this.

  LIMITATIONS THAT REMAIN, stated so they are not mistaken for results:
   * pH is not a state.  The Bohr effect and the strong pH dependence of both
     BPGM activities are folded into fixed constants, so acid-base disturbance
     cannot be simulated.
   * Free versus Mg-bound ADP is not resolved; the PGK equilibrium uses total
     ADP, which overstates how far ATP/ADP falls.
   * The 3-PG inhibition constant of the 2,3-BPG phosphatase (30 uM) is the
     single most load-bearing parameter for the 2,3-BPG magnitude and it is the
     least well pinned by data.  The oxygen-transport conclusion in Section 3
     depends on the 2,3-BPG excursion, so it inherits that uncertainty; the
     break-even table is given precisely so the conclusion can be re-scored
     against a different 2,3-BPG estimate.
   * Splenic destruction and sequestration are one compartment with a
     mean-field transit; there is no distribution of transit times.
   * The oxygen module is whole-body and single-tissue.  It cannot represent
     regional differences in extraction, which is where a right-shifted curve
     actually helps or hurts most.
   * Cardiac output responds to haemoglobin, not to tissue PO2, so the model
     cannot arbitrate between the three closures of Section 3 -- it reports all
     of them instead.
   * Only three parameters were fitted (tau_PK of the mutant, and the two
     haemolysis gains) plus the single marrow-output scalar.  Everything else is
     literature or back-calculated from the normal operating point.  That is a
     claim about provenance, not about correctness.
