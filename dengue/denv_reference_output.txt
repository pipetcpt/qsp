   Wills 2005 NEJM randomised 512 children and found NO overall difference
   between Ringer's lactate, dextran-70 and 6 % starch, but a colloid
   advantage confined to the narrowest-pulse-pressure stratum.  The model
   reproduces that pattern without being told: the colloid margin is small
   and grows monotonically with the depth of the sigma defect, because the
   oncotic term it adds enters the Starling equation multiplied by sigma.

L. DEFERVESCENCE AND LEAK ONSET ARE ONE EVENT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   peak viraemia                          t =    17.5 h
   peak NS1                               t =    23.0 h
   steepest rise of neutralising IgG      t =    59.5 h
   peak immune-complex load               t =    40.0 h
   defervescence (T < 37.8 C)             t =    89.5 h
   peak serosal leak (J_ser - drainage)   t =    44.0 h   (24 mL/h)
   peak haematocrit                       t =    69.0 h   (50.7 %)
   narrowest pulse pressure               t =    68.0 h   (10.8 mmHg)
   -> narrowest pulse pressure minus defervescence = -21.5 h;
      peak haematocrit minus defervescence          = -20.5 h;
      peak serosal filtration rate minus defervescence = -45.5 h.
   Temperature is computed from hypothalamic PGE2 driven by interferon;
   the leak is computed from a glycocalyx driven by NS1, TNF, VEGF and
   chymase.  The two chains share no equation after the antibody rise,
   yet the clinical nadir and the fever break land within a day of each
   other, because both are that antibody rise seen from a different side.

L2. WHY PROPHYLACTIC PLATELET TRANSFUSION DOES NOTHING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   platelet nadir      32  ->  44 x10^9/L (+12 after three pools)
   bleeding index      0.490  ->  0.444
   bleeding sub-score  0.619  ->  0.534
   severity            0.249  ->  0.237
   Haemostasis is written as a PRODUCT of four requirements -- platelet
   count, fibrinogen, vessel-wall integrity and perfusion -- so the worst
   term sets the answer.  At the nadir the vessel-wall term is the worst,
   and platelets are not the vessel wall.  AAPT (Lye 2017, Lancet) found
   prophylactic transfusion did not prevent bleeding; the model agrees for
   a structural reason rather than by fitting the trial.
