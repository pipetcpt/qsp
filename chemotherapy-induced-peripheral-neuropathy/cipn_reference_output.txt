cipn_reference_model.py
=======================
Dependency-free (stdlib-only) reference implementation of the CIPN QSP model.

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
--------------------
The deliverables in this directory are `cipn_mrgsolve_model.R` (the model of
record) and `cipn_shiny_app.R` (the dashboard).  Neither can be executed in a
plain Python environment, so every quantitative statement in `README.md` would
otherwise be an assertion rather than a result.  This file re-implements the
*same* ODE system with the *same* parameter values in pure Python + `math`, and
prints every number quoted in the README.  Run it with:

    python3 cipn_reference_model.py

   - grade >=3 is systematically UNDER-predicted (FOLFOX 3 mo 0.8% vs

     distribution is too narrow at the top: a single lognormal
     susceptibility cannot widen the upper tail without also
     inflating grade >=2, which is pinned by the fit.

     DIRECTION of the schedule benefit right but exaggerates it,
     because inter-dose recovery of MITO (t1/2 4.6 d) and axonal
     transport (t1/2 6.9 d) is close to complete over 7 days.
   - paclitaxel q3w is under-predicted (14.9% vs 20%), i.e. the model
     attributes slightly too much of the taxane schedule effect to
     cumulative dose and too little to peak concentration.

==============================================================================
  FOLFOX 6 months            last dose day   154   peak severity day   187   coasting =  33.0 d ( 4.7 weeks)
  FOLFOX 3 months            last dose day    70   peak severity day   136   coasting =  66.0 d ( 9.4 weeks)

  Bortezomib IV x8           last dose day   168   peak severity day   168   coasting =   0.0 d ( 0.0 weeks)

  Mechanism: at the last dose the SARM1 axon-death program is already
  switched on and its off-rate is t1/2 = 23 d, while DRG platinum
  washes out with t1/2 = 8 d - so degeneration outlives exposure.
  FOLFOX: SARM1 activity at last dose (d154) = 0.098; at peak severity = 0.062; at d365 = 0.000
  AXON at last dose = 82.2%  ->  at peak = 79.5%  (a further 2.7 percentage points lost with NO further drug)

5. BORTEZOMIB SC vs IV: SAME AUC, ~1/14 THE Cmax  (both arms FITTED)
==============================================================================
  IV: model grade>=2 PN =  41.0%   observed 41% (FIT)     model grade>=3 =  7.0%
  SC: model grade>=2 PN =  23.8%   observed 24% (FIT)     model grade>=3 =  2.5%

    peak DRG 20S occupancy   IV  30.9%   SC   8.1%   ratio  3.8x
    AUC of occupancy (d)     IV  0.0050  SC  0.0059  ratio  0.9x
    AUC of INJURY flux J     IV  0.0057  SC  0.0048  ratio  1.2x

  READ THOSE THREE LINES CAREFULLY. The DRG occupancy AUC actually
  slightly FAVOURS the IV route (0.85x, i.e. SC has MORE occupancy-time,
  because occupancy saturates and the low flat SC profile spends longer
  in the linear region). Nothing about total exposure explains the
  toxicity difference. But the injury flux is a Hill function (h = 1.51)
  of occupancy, so the brief IV spike delivers 1.19x the INJURY. Convexity,
  not exposure, is the entire mechanism - and it costs nothing in
  efficacy, because tumour kill depends on the saturating BLOOD
  occupancy AUC, which the two routes share exactly.

  because the taxane nerve burden integrates cumulative dose (960 vs
  700 mg/m2) and 7-day gaps leave less inter-cycle recovery than 21-day
  gaps. It over-separates them (27 vs 14.9 rather than 27 vs 20), i.e.
  it attributes slightly too much of the effect to cumulative dose.

        AXON0   = 100.0  grade>=2 =  47.7%   relative risk 1.00
        AXON0   =  90.0  grade>=2 =  44.1%   relative risk 0.93
        AXON0   =  80.0  grade>=2 =  40.1%   relative risk 0.84
        AXON0   =  70.0  grade>=2 =  35.4%   relative risk 0.74

      THE MOST USEFUL THING THIS MODEL SAYS ABOUT PATIENTS:
      a 6% fall in bioenergetic reserve DOUBLES the risk; a 45% fall in
      regenerative capacity moves it by 9%; and pre-existing subclinical
      fibre loss, on its own, does not raise treatment-emergent CIPN at
      all. Peak severity is set by the ENERGY set-point during exposure.
      Regeneration governs how much comes back, not how bad it gets, and
      a low starting fibre count is not itself a risk factor once the
      grading is done - as in the clinic - relative to the patient's own
      baseline. So the reason diabetics fare worse should be their
      mitochondrial state, NOT their nerve-conduction study; and the
      therapeutic implication is that protecting bioenergetics before
      and during treatment dominates anything that speeds regrowth after.
      CAVEAT: the leverage on RESERVE is almost certainly too high.
      Diabetes carries an observed relative risk near 1.5-2 and the model
      needs only a 10% reserve deficit to produce that, so RESERVE is the
      single parameter most in need of real data before this model is
      used to predict an individual patient.

  grade>=3  end-of-tx  10.2%  12 mo   0.1%  24 mo   0.0%  48 mo   0.0%
  grade>=2  end-of-tx  46.3%  12 mo   2.5%  24 mo   0.7%  48 mo   0.7%
  grade>=1  end-of-tx  79.6%  12 mo  27.9%  24 mo   7.8%  48 mo   7.8%
  observed  grade 3: 12.4% during tx, 1.1% at 12 mo, 0.7% at 48 mo (MOSAIC)

    day   154: AXON  82.2%  NEURON  99.0%  IENFD 5.76/mm  CIPN20  22.7  NfL   40.0 pg/mL
    day   210: AXON  81.6%  NEURON  98.8%  IENFD 5.71/mm  CIPN20  23.9  NfL   25.7 pg/mL
    day   365: AXON  96.2%  NEURON  98.8%  IENFD 6.74/mm  CIPN20   6.8  NfL   10.2 pg/mL
    day   730: AXON  98.8%  NEURON  98.8%  IENFD 6.91/mm  CIPN20   2.4  NfL   10.0 pg/mL
    day  1460: AXON  98.8%  NEURON  98.8%  IENFD 6.91/mm  CIPN20   2.4  NfL   10.0 pg/mL

  THIS IS THE MODEL'S CLEAREST FAILURE, and it is worth stating
  precisely because the diagnosis is unambiguous. The permanent floor
  is set by irreversible DRG NEURON loss, and at the median
  susceptibility only 1.2% of somata are lost, so AXON returns to
  98.8% and the model recovers almost completely. Reality does
  not: roughly a third of patients still have grade >=1 sensory
  symptoms five years after adjuvant FOLFOX (Selvy 2020), and MOSAIC
  still saw 0.7% grade 3 at four years. The model gives 7.8% and 0.0%.

  The same thin tail explains the grade >=3 under-prediction in section
  0b - both are the upper/persistent end of the same distribution. One
  parameter would fix both: KNEURON, the rate of irreversible DRG
  neuron death. Raising it deepens the permanent floor AND fattens the
  severe tail. The reason it is not simply raised here is that nothing
  pins it: there is no human dataset quantifying DRG neuron loss after
  platinum, so fitting it would mean fitting the persistence data
  twice - once as calibration and once as validation. The honest
  conclusion is that the parameter which dominates the long-term
  outcome of CIPN is the one nobody has measured.

9. PLASMA NfL AS AN EARLY WARNING (lead time over the clinical score)
==============================================================================
  baseline NfL = 10.0 pg/mL, peak = 40.7 pg/mL (4.1x baseline)
  NfL doubles on day            80  (cycle 6)
  CTCAE grade 1 reached day    100
  CTCAE grade 2 reached day   never
  -> NfL lead time over grade 1 = 20 days (1.4 cycles of oxaliplatin)

  This is the model's most actionable output. The MEDIAN patient never
  reaches grade 2 at all, yet their NfL doubles part-way through
  treatment - so NfL is not a surrogate for the grade, it is a signal
  that axons are being lost in a patient the CTCAE scale still calls
  normal. Combined with section 11, that is a decision rule: the
  anti-tumour exposure-response is already ~2/3 saturated at 510 mg/m2,
  so stopping oxaliplatin when NfL rises costs very little tumour kill.

11. THE THERAPEUTIC-INDEX OPTIMUM IN CUMULATIVE OXALIPLATIN DOSE
==============================================================================
  Net utility = (3-yr DFS gain over no-oxaliplatin, pp)
                - w x (grade>=2 CIPN incidence, pp),  w = 0.15

  cycles  cum mg/m2   g>=2 %  DFS-hi %  DFS-lo %  util-hi  util-lo
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
       2        170      0.0      59.1      80.4     5.88     3.42
       4        340      3.5      61.4      81.7     7.72     4.22
       6        510     16.6      62.7      82.4     7.01     2.94
       8        680     31.2      63.5      82.8     5.60     1.18
      10        850     41.6      64.0      83.1     4.58    -0.09
      12       1020     47.7      64.4      83.3     4.05    -0.80
      14       1190     53.7      64.7      83.4     3.45    -1.54

  Utility-maximising dose: high risk 4 cycles (340 mg/m2), low risk 4 cycles (340 mg/m2).

  The interesting result is NOT that the two optima differ - at this
  weight they coincide, because the CIPN cost curve is identical in
  both strata and only the available DFS gain differs. It is the
  ASYMMETRY OF OVER-TREATMENT. Going from the optimum to a full 12
  cycles costs the high-risk patient 3.67 utility points but leaves
  them ahead of no oxaliplatin at all; it takes the LOW-risk patient
  BELOW zero, i.e. a low-risk patient given the full 12 cycles is worse
  off on this metric than one given none, because the last 500 mg/m2
  buys ~0.5 pp of DFS and ~31 pp of grade >=2 neuropathy.
  That asymmetry is exactly why IDEA's non-inferiority held in low-risk
  disease and was not established in high-risk - and here it is derived
  from an exposure-response that saturates, not assumed.

  Sensitivity to the weight w placed on CIPN burden:
    w = 0.05   high risk -> 12 cycles   low risk ->  6 cycles
    w = 0.10   high risk ->  4 cycles   low risk ->  4 cycles
    w = 0.15   high risk ->  4 cycles   low risk ->  4 cycles
    w = 0.30   high risk ->  4 cycles   low risk ->  4 cycles

  THIS IS THE RESULT WORTH KEEPING. At w = 0.05 - CIPN counted at one
  twentieth of a DFS point - the two strata SEPARATE, and they separate
  onto exactly the IDEA recommendation: 12 cycles (6 months) for high
  risk, 6 cycles (3 months) for low risk. Nothing in the model was told
  about trial duration; the split falls out of a saturating anti-tumour
  exposure-response meeting a linearly accumulating neurotoxicity, with
  the crossover set by how much residual disease there is to kill.
  Weight CIPN more heavily and both strata collapse to 4 cycles - i.e.
  for a patient who fears neuropathy more than the trialists did, even
  3 months is longer than optimal. The clinical reading is that the
  3-vs-6-month question has no answer independent of how the patient
  weights the two harms, and this model turns that weight into a dose.

  Caveat on the criterion, not the biology: a single linear weight on
  grade >=2 INCIDENCE ignores severity, duration and reversibility - all
  of which the model tracks and could be weighted instead.
