The three terms are not interchangeable.  P_cv appears BOTH in the
pressure balance and (through P_seg) in the collapse drive, so its
net coefficient is close to 1 -- it is NOT amplified.  Anything
entering through R_out*F_form is amplified by 1/(1-g).

open-loop delivery of exactly 1.00 mmHg, measured at BMI 38:
  through P_cv (weight, posture, jugular)   dICP = +1.000 mmHg
  through R_out*F_form (drug, outflow)      dICP = +2.130 mmHg
  ratio = 2.13   (1/(1-g) = 2.13)

sensitivity to FSEG, the position along the stenosis at which the
collapse is driven (0.5 = mid-segment, the default):
FSEG      ICP(cm)         g P_cv coef  CSF coef
0.00         31.2     0.375     1.000     1.570
0.25         32.6     0.452     1.000     1.787
0.50         35.1     0.530     1.000     2.130
0.75         38.7     0.473     1.000     2.067
1.00         41.1     0.256     1.000     1.416

The unamplified status of body weight is a consequence of FSEG=0.5;
if the collapse were driven at the jugular end (FSEG=0) weight would
be amplified too.  This is the model's single largest structural
uncertainty and it is recorded here rather than hidden.

ANALYSIS 3 -- where the fixed point folds -- and the finding that the trial-calibrated patient is NOT there
==============================================================================
The loop can in principle destroy its own low-pressure solution.
Whether it does is decided by one number: the maximum slope of the
collapse map, which for a logistic collapse is

    h'max = FSEG * Q_v * RTSCMAX / (4 * PSTIFF)

and multiplicity requires h'max > 1.  At the calibrated values
    FSEG 0.50, Q_v 600, RTSCMAX 0.0200, PSTIFF 4.14  ->  h'max = 0.362

so h'max < 1 and the calibrated patient has EXACTLY ONE stable
pressure at every BMI.  This is a result, not an omission: the
parameters were fixed by three trial anchors before this analysis was
run, and they place the typical patient safely below the fold.

Read the last two columns.  In the monostable phenotype the pressure
returns to where it started, so the drug is a parameter shift that
must be maintained -- which is what is observed in ordinary IIH and
why relapse on withdrawal is the rule.  A fold, when it exists, is
what would make a finite course curative, and the model says the
sinus has to be roughly three times more compliant than the
trial-calibrated value before that becomes possible.  That is a
falsifiable statement about WHICH patients could ever stop.

Rank the rows by mean deviation and by headache and you get two

disc reads the DIFFERENCE.  ONSF also does nothing for the fellow
eye, which in this model is not a limitation of the operation but an
identity: the fellow nerve still sees the undiminished ICP.

is the MEASURABLE venous gradient a usable proxy for the
UNMEASURABLE loop gain that actually sets drug response?

  r(g, transverse sinus gradient) = -0.398
  r(g, opening pressure)          = -0.410
  r(gradient, opening pressure)   = +0.952

  The answer is NO, and the sign is the interesting part.  ANALYSIS 1
  showed that g is NOT monotone in disease severity: it peaks around
  BMI 36-38 and falls again as the collapse saturates.  In a cohort
  spanning that peak, the gradient and the gain therefore run in
  OPPOSITE directions over part of the range, and the linear
  correlation comes out negative.  The clinically attractive shortcut
  -- 'measure the gradient, infer how much the drug will do' -- is
  refuted by the same structure that makes the gradient worth
  measuring for stent selection.  Two different questions, two
  different variables.
