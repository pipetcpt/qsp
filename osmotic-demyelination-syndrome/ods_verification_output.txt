5. PROACTIVE DESMOPRESSIN CLAMP — and what happens when it comes off
==============================================================================
  Same hypovolaemic patient.  Desmopressin 2 ug IV q8h for five days plus
  3% saline titrated to +6 mmol/L/24 h, against 0.9% saline alone.  The
  correction rate is reported in TWO windows because the model found
  something in the second one that was not designed in.

                                           d0-5   d4-14  TBW d5  maxSTR  deficit
  0.9% saline alone                        20.9     0.0    43.8   14.77     60.0
  clamp, fluid intake 1.5 L/d               6.0    17.1    46.8   16.94     41.2
  clamp, fluid intake 1.0 L/d               6.0     9.5    45.0   10.15      0.0
  clamp, fluid intake 0.5 L/d               6.2     4.8    42.4    4.56      0.0
  clamp 1.0 L/d + dose taper 2->0.25 ug     6.0     9.7    45.0   10.34      0.0

  INSIDE the clamp the prescription and the outcome are the same number:
  6.0 mmol/L/24 h against a prescribed 6.0, in every arm, with no injury.
  The clamp does not lower the sodium; it takes the KIDNEY out of the loop.

  TAKING IT OFF is the other half of the therapy, and the model says the
  rebound is a STORED-WATER problem, not a tapering problem:
   - at 1.5 L/d of intake the patient stores water to 46.8 L, and when the
     drug clears the sodium rebounds 17.1 mmol/L in 24 h — peak stress 16.9,
     peak deficit 41.2.  The clamp caused the injury it was preventing.
   - at 1.0 L/d: 45.0 L stored, rebound 9.5, deficit 0.0.
   - at 0.5 L/d: 42.4 L stored, rebound 4.8, peak stress 4.6 — never near threshold.
   - tapering the DOSE from 2 ug to 0.25 ug changes nothing (9.7 vs 9.5),
     because 0.25 ug still gives about 10 pg/mL against a V2 EC50 of 1.6:
     the receptor stays saturated until the drug is essentially gone,
     whatever the schedule.  The aquaresis is not tapering; it is waiting.

  So the derived rule is not "wean the desmopressin".  It is: while the
  clamp is on, hold the free-water balance neutral, because every litre
  retained is a litre that will be excreted the moment it comes off.

9. UREA AND THE HONEST NEGATIVE RESULT
==============================================================================
  Under a pure effective-tonicity accounting urea CANNOT protect the
  brain: it crosses the blood-brain barrier, so it appears on both sides
  of the balance and cancels exactly.  The animal data (Soupart 2000,
  Gankam Kengne 2009) are real, so in this model urea acts where those
  studies MEASURED an effect — the blood-brain barrier and microglia —
  and not on the osmotic arm.  That is a falsifiable structural claim.

                                      maxSTRESS  BBB peak  astro nadir  deficit
  overcorrection, no urea                 14.76     1.202        0.686     60.0
  overcorrection + urea 30 g/d            13.73     1.171        0.667     57.7
  overcorrection + dexamethasone          14.76     1.111        0.686     55.1
  overcorrection + minocycline            14.76     1.202        0.686     54.4

  Note the pattern: STRESS is IDENTICAL in all four arms (the osmotic
  insult is untouched) and only the downstream arms move.  If a trial ever
  showed urea blunting the osmotic insult itself, this model is wrong.

10. RATE versus TOTAL MAGNITUDE
==============================================================================
  Is 8 mmol/L/day for four days safer than 12 in one day and then stop?
  Same patient, same 32 mmol/L of total correction either way.

  +8/d x 4 d   (32 total)    24h rise   8.1  48h rise  15.4  maxSTRESS  5.94  deficit   0.0
  +12 on d1 then hold        24h rise  11.1  48h rise  10.0  maxSTRESS  7.60  deficit   0.0
  +16 on d1 then hold        24h rise  14.9  48h rise  13.9  maxSTRESS 10.25  deficit   1.1
  +6/d x 5 d   (30 total)    24h rise   6.0  48h rise  11.5  maxSTRESS  4.58  deficit   0.0

  The 24 h rate is not sufficient on its own: Omega depends on the gap
  between the CURRENT tonicity and an osmolyte pool with a 2-3 day time
  constant, so a second and third day of "safe" correction keep adding to
  a deficit the brain has not yet closed.  The 48 h limit in the guidelines
  is not a separate rule here; it is the same rule read one day later.
