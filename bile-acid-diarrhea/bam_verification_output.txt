9. TRANSIT-ABSORPTION POSITIVE FEEDBACK — is it strong enough to latch?
============================================================================================
Colonic bile acid accelerates transit; faster transit shortens ileal
contact time and spills more bile acid.  That is a genuine POSITIVE
feedback loop.  The question is quantitative: is its gain above 1?

The loop is OPENED by clamping TRANS, and the two halves are measured
separately:
   A:  d(Ds)/d(TRANS)        -- how much extra spillage faster transit buys
   B:  d(TRANSss)/d(Ds)      -- how much extra transit that spillage buys
   loop gain L = A x B  (dimensionless).  L >= 1 would permit latching.

   phi    aTR  Ds@T=1.00  Ds@T=1.10        A        B   L = AxB  verdict
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1.00   2.40      1.246      1.181   -0.649    0.422   -0.2739  stable (no hysteresis)
  1.00   3.60      1.246      1.181   -0.649    0.633   -0.4109  stable (no hysteresis)
  0.30   1.55      3.766      3.494   -2.722    0.252   -0.6867  stable (no hysteresis)

  0.30   3.60      3.766      3.494   -2.722    0.586   -1.5950  stable (no hysteresis)

HONEST NEGATIVE RESULT.  Over the whole physiological range the loop
gain stays well below 1, so the model does NOT reproduce a latching,
self-sustaining state, and every transient insult resolves completely.
The transit arm therefore AMPLIFIES bile acid diarrhoea but cannot
CAUSE it, and post-infectious bile acid diarrhoea cannot be explained
by this loop alone -- it needs a persisting lesion in phi or kappa.
That is a constraint the model imposes, not one that was put into it.
