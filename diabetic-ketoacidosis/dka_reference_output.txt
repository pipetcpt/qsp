    ketoanion RETAINED at presentation (= potential bicarbonate) : 437 mmol
    ketoanion excreted with Na+/K+ during treatment (base LOST)  : 17 mmol
    ketoanion excreted with NH4+ during treatment (base KEPT)    : 13 mmol
    chloride infused                                             : 625 mmol
    -> at 24 h: HCO3 20.3, Cl 109.4, anion gap 11.2, pH 7.368

  Of those entries the ketoanion already lost BEFORE arrival is the
  larger one: 102 mmol, which over a 35 L bicarbonate space is 2.9
  mmol/L of base that cannot be recovered by oxidising anything, because
  the carbon carrying it has left the body.
  Removing the chloride load alone (balanced crystalloid) is worth
  +3.6 mmol/L at 24 h, taking HCO3 from 20.3 to 23.8 against a normal
  24.9; the residual 1.1 mmol/L is the ketoanion ledger, and no fluid
  choice touches it.  Renal ammoniagenesis is the only route back and it
  is capacity-limited and slow (2.2 mmol/h at 24 h against a ceiling of
  12, tau 12 h), which is why the repair takes days, not hours.
  Impose AKI at the start of treatment and the same arms give HCO3 20.4
  (saline) and 24.1 (balanced) -- but the anion gap closes 4.0 h EARLIER,
  because a ketoanion that cannot be filtered away has to be OXIDISED --
  and oxidising it regenerates the bicarbonate that excreting it loses.
  A failing kidney therefore makes the anion gap look better and the base
  ledger worse, which is the opposite of the intuition.
  (Ringer's lactate also raises MEASURED lactate to 2.6 mmol/L at 4 h,
  which is not tissue hypoxia and should not be treated as such.)

  The interesting number is the TROUGH, and it needs two columns.
  Between q2h doses the PLASMA insulin falls to 13 uU/mL, i.e. 0.89 of
  the 15 uU/mL lipolysis IC50, because plasma insulin has a 5-minute

  intermittent subcutaneous protocol can suppress ketogenesis at all, and
  it also says where it breaks: lengthen the interval, or slow absorption
  (shock, oedema, regular insulin at ka 0.5 instead of 1.5 /h), and it
  is the effect-site trough, not the peak, that crosses the constant.
