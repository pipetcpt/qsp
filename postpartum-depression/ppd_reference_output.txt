TABLE 1 · The two arms of the product, and why a trough exists
===============================================================================
Potentiation is Emax*PAM^h/(EC50^h+PAM^h) with Emax=2.50, EC50=120 nM, h=1.4
The delta-pool set-point is R_ss = G_target/(1+potentiation), so the
PRODUCT is held at G_target = 1.00 in ANY steady state.

state                           PAM(nM) potentiation   R_delta,ss   G_tonic,ss
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
non-pregnant                        6.0        0.037        0.964        1.000
2nd trimester                      60.0        0.687        0.593        1.000
term (3rd trimester)              160.0        1.498        0.400        1.000
day 3 postpartum                    4.8        0.027        0.973        1.000
day 5 postpartum                    4.0        0.021        0.979        1.000

The steady states are all identical (1.00) — the disease is NOT a
steady state.  It is the TRANSIENT between two of them:
   R_delta is still 0.400 (the term value) when PAM has already
   reached 4.8 nM  ->  G_tonic = 0.411  =  a 59% LOSS of tonic
   inhibition that no steady-state analysis would ever show.

   tonic-inhibition deficit stays worse than 25 %  for    149 h =  6.2 d
   tonic-inhibition deficit stays worse than 10 %  for    308 h = 12.8 d
   tonic-inhibition deficit stays worse than 5 %   for    428 h = 17.8 d

=> the model DERIVES a 2-3 week window of reduced tonic inhibition
   from two time constants (ligand t1/2 12 h, receptor t1/2 120 h)
   and nothing else.  Compare with the observed clustering of PPD
   onset in the first postpartum weeks (PMID 23487258).  The width
   is set by the RECEPTOR constant, which has no human measurement
   (assumption A8) — so treat the width as an order of magnitude,
   not a prediction.

TABLE 4 · Brexanolone: PK, saturation, and the flat 60->90 arm
===============================================================================
rate ug/kg/h    Css ng/mL     Css nM  brain nM-eq potentiation      vs 30
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
30                   30.0       94.2          188        1.632       1.00
60                   60.0      188.4          377        2.081       1.27
90                   90.0      282.6          565        2.244       1.37
120                 120.0      376.8          754        2.323       1.42

Going from 60 to 90 ug/kg/h raises steady-state exposure by 50 %
but potentiation by only 7.8 % — the Hill function is already
saturated.  Site-2 direct activation (sedation) has no such ceiling
in this range.  A flat 60-vs-90 efficacy comparison with MORE
sedation at the higher rate is therefore the PREDICTION, and it is
what phase 3 study 1 reported (-19.5 for BRX60 vs -17.7 for BRX90).

Steady-state brexanolone exposure at 90 ug/kg/h is 283 nM plasma,
i.e. 3.5x the third-trimester allopregnanolone level the model uses
(80 nM).  The infusion does not 'restore pregnancy levels' — it
overshoots them severalfold, which is exactly why sedation, not
efficacy, is the dose-limiting toxicity.

WHERE THIS FAILS — state it plainly:
  The model reproduces (a) the enrolment severity, (b) the 60-hour
  brexanolone response, (c) the day-15 zuranolone response and
  (d) the magnitude of both placebo responses.  It does NOT
  reproduce the MAINTENANCE of drug-placebo separation at days
  28-45: every arm converges to about HAM-D 14, whereas the trials
  kept a small separation to day 30 (brexanolone) and day 45
  (zuranolone).  The late-time attractor of this system is a
  partially-recovered state that all arms fall into once the
  receptor trough has closed, and no drug parameter changes where
  that attractor sits — only how fast a patient reaches it.
  Table 12 shows that the two obvious mechanistic repairs fail, and
  why the failure is structural rather than a fitting problem.

TABLE 6 · The bridge test: is durability the receptor, or the drug?
===============================================================================
Brexanolone stops at 60 h.  Zuranolone stops at day 14.  Both keep
working.  The model says the drug does not hold the improvement —
it holds the patient until R_delta has re-expanded, and the
symptom-sleep loop then keeps her in the recovered state.
So switch OFF receptor recovery (KR = 0) and re-run.

arm                                   d 3       d 15       d 45
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
brexanolone, KR normal              -19.8      -13.7      -15.1
zuranolone 50 mg, KR normal          -7.4      -15.5      -14.7
brexanolone, KR = 0                 -16.5      -11.8      -11.9
zuranolone 50 mg, KR = 0             -6.8      -13.7      -11.9

With the receptor frozen, the ON-DRUG response survives (the ligand
arm still works) but the day-45 benefit is 3-4 HAM-D points worse in
BOTH arms.  No parameter of the symptom equations was touched, so
this is a structural consequence: part of what looks like drug
durability is receptor recovery that would have happened anyway.
It is NOT the whole story — see the failure noted under Table 5.

Course length at the same daily dose (zuranolone 50 mg):
  3-day course   day 15  -12.8   day 45  -15.0
  14-day course  day 15  -15.5   day 45  -14.7

NOTE — the course-length prediction does NOT come out: the 3-day
course is worse at day 15 (it has stopped) but indistinguishable at
day 45, because by then every arm has reached the same attractor.
So this model does not support 'shorter courses relapse'.  It
supports only the weaker claim that a course must still be running
to hold an acute advantage.  A model that reproduced maintained
separation would be needed to say anything about course length, and
this is not that model.

TABLE 7 · Why the placebo response is so large, and so variable
===============================================================================
The placebo arms of these trials improved by 8.8 to 14.0 HAM-D
points.  In this model that is not noise: an enrolled woman is
sitting on a receptor pool that is still re-expanding, and trial
participation itself changes her sleep.  Both are dated quantities,
so the model predicts placebo response should SHRINK with later
enrolment.

enrolment           R_delta    G_tonic  placebo 60h  placebo d15
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
day 7                 0.661      0.647        -15.5        -12.5
day 14                0.834      0.829        -15.7        -12.9
day 21                0.911      0.912        -15.6        -13.1
day 42                0.968      0.972        -15.1        -12.9
day 90                0.973      0.976        -14.7        -12.5
day 180               0.973      0.960        -14.8        -12.4

TWO readings, and only one of them supports the model:
  SUPPORTED — the inpatient 60-hour column is larger than the
  outpatient day-15 column at every enrolment day, and for a
  mechanistic reason: continuous nursing care protects sleep, which
  is a state variable here.  The brexanolone trials were inpatient
  and their placebo arms improved -14.0 in 60 h; the zuranolone
  trials were outpatient and theirs took 15 days to reach -11.6.
  The model reproduces that ordering without being told about it.
  NOT SUPPORTED — the predicted placebo response is nearly FLAT in
  enrolment day (about 1 HAM-D point across day 7 to day 180), even
  though the delta-pool column moves from 0.66 to 0.97.  The
  calibrated care term dominates the mechanistic one.  So the model
  does not, after calibration, predict a useful enrolment-timing
  effect, and the earlier hypothesis that receptor recovery explains
  much of the placebo response is NOT borne out at these weights.

Sertraline is the one arm that keeps improving past day 28 in this
model, because its slow plasticity arm keeps pushing after the
receptor trough has closed.  That is a model artefact worth naming:
it comes from SYN entering the excitatory load as a divisor with no
ceiling, and it should not be read as a claim that sertraline beats
a neurosteroid at week 6.

Nothing about sertraline was made weak: its SERT occupancy is
immediate (78 % at day 15) and its final effect is competitive.
The delay comes from two states in series — 5-HT1A autoreceptor
desensitisation (t1/2 10 d) and structural plasticity (t1/2 10 d)
— which is why a GABA_A PAM and an SSRI can converge by week 6 and
still be unrecognisably different on day 3.

TABLE 9 · Maternal versus infant exposure during lactation
===============================================================================
regimen                      Cmax mat milk ng/mL      RID %  infant nM
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
brexanolone 90 (60 h)            88.1      132.2       0.65     0.5010
zuranolone 50 mg/d              246.3      369.5       5.47     1.1547
sertraline 100 mg/d              54.5       98.2       0.80     0.3321

The relative infant dose is what matters, not the maternal dose.
The 60-hour brexanolone infusion delivers well under 1 %% of the
maternal dose per kg, against a measured brexanolone milk study
(PMID 35869362) that this column should be checked against; the
14-day oral zuranolone course delivers several-fold more because it
is given for 14 days rather than 60 hours, and still sits under the
conventional 10 %% concern threshold.
An orally dosed neurosteroid is additionally crippled by first-pass
metabolism (F_oral = 0.05 assumed here, with NO neonatal
measurement behind it), so the infant plasma concentrations above
are ~2 orders of magnitude below the model's own potentiation EC50
of 120 nM.  Maternal sedation and infant sedation are different
questions and this model separates them explicitly rather than
reasoning from the maternal dose.

    symptom loop behaves inside a window that is open either way.

TABLE 12 · Two mechanistic repairs, both rejected
===============================================================================
The model under-predicts maintained drug-placebo separation
(Table 5).  The two obvious repairs were implemented as switches and
tested rather than argued about.

REPAIR 1 (KR_BOOST): let PAM exposure ACCELERATE delta-pool
plasticity, so the drug speeds up the recovery it is bridging.
REPAIR 2 (W_PAM_BDNF): give the PAM a plasticity arm through BDNF,
so restoring tonic inhibition drives structural recovery.

setting                        base  brx 60h  brx d30  zur d15
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
reference (both off)           28.8    -19.2    -14.4    -15.5
KR_BOOST = 1.0                 28.7    -17.9    -14.2    -14.0
KR_BOOST = 3.0                 28.7    -16.7    -14.1    -13.3
W_PAM_BDNF = 0.15              25.5    -17.2    -11.7    -14.6
W_PAM_BDNF = 0.35              23.9    -16.2    -10.7    -16.6

BOTH REPAIRS MAKE THE FIT WORSE, AND FOR THE SAME REASON.
Brexanolone IS allopregnanolone (assumption A2 — a fact, not a
modelling choice), so the drug and the endogenous ligand enter the
model through the SAME potentiation term.  Any parameter that gives
the drug extra credit also gives PREGNANCY extra credit: it lowers
the baseline the patient starts from and shrinks the room a drug has
to work in.  The 'base' column above shows exactly that (28.8 ->
25.5 -> 23.9 as W_PAM_BDNF rises).  REPAIR 1 fails even more
directly: in a homeostatic set-point model a PAM cannot accelerate
recovery of the pool, because it LOWERS the set-point the pool is
chasing.  Speeding up the chase moves it toward a lower target.

So the missing durability mechanism cannot live on the
allopregnanolone-potentiation axis at all.  It has to be something
the drug does that pregnancy does not — a candidate the trials could
distinguish: a durable change in the sleep/behaviour loop bought by
60 hours of functioning (which this model would represent as a
lasting reduction in A_SYMP or WAKE, not as a receptor effect).
