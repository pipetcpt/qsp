[7] BISPHOSPHONATE: WHAT IS THE MECHANISTIC CEILING?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Alendronate acts on ONE limb of the interface: it suppresses the
  osteoclastic excavation that makes the interface porous.  It cannot
  make bone.  And because osteoblast recruitment in this model follows
  the resorbed surface (the coupling term), suppressing resorption also
  removes part of the stimulus for the formation it is protecting.

     cannot produce Lai's effect size at ANY degree of osteoclast
     blockade, because blockade cannot lift beta above the value it
     would have had with no resorption at all.  It sits close to Chen.

[13] THE HAZARD IS NOT MONOTONE  (a prediction of clock 2, not a fit)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Because the interface loses strength only while the front is crossing
  and regains it afterwards, the collapse hazard has to rise, peak and
  then vanish.  Conditional probability of collapsing in the NEXT year,
  given the hip is still intact now:
     intact at     0 months (n= 360)  ->  collapse within the next 12 months: 0.294
     intact at    12 months (n= 254)  ->  collapse within the next 12 months: 0.051
     intact at    24 months (n= 241)  ->  collapse within the next 12 months: 0.012
     intact at    36 months (n= 238)  ->  collapse within the next 12 months: 0.008
     intact at    48 months (n= 236)  ->  collapse within the next 12 months: 0.004
  Clinically: the great majority of collapses occur inside two years,
  and a head still spherical at four years is very unlikely to fail.
  Nothing in the model was fitted to that; it follows from the fact
  that creeping substitution finishes.
