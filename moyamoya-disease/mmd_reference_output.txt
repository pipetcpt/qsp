3.  ACETAZOLAMIDE AND CO2 ARE NOT THE SAME PROBE
==============================================================================
  Acetazolamide acts on the arteriole (tissue carbonic anhydrase).
  PaCO2 acts on the arteriole AND on the pial collateral conduits.
  In a normal brain that distinction is invisible.  In moyamoya the
  conduits ARE the circulation, so the two probes disagree.

             state  CVRi%  CBF_A |   ACZ 1 g    dA%    dB% |  PaCO2 50    dA% |  PaCO2 25    dA%
       normal (y1)   40.6   50.0 |      69.1   38.1   39.0 |      67.5   35.0 |      24.1  -51.8
  reserve lost (y4)    0.0   40.0 |      39.3   -1.7   39.0 |      41.8    4.4 |      33.0  -17.5
  decompensated (y6)    0.0   39.5 |      38.8   -1.9   39.0 |      42.0    6.2 |      31.6  -20.0
   end-stage (y10)    0.0   39.6 |      38.8   -2.0   39.0 |      42.3    6.8 |      31.5  -20.5

  The donor territory answers acetazolamide every time.  The
  affected territory stops answering, then answers with the WRONG
  SIGN -- because territory B is territory A's supply, so dilating B
  steals from A.  The steal is a property of the network, not an
  extra assumption.  Hypercapnia, by contrast, opens the conduits
  themselves and helps -- which is why normocapnia-to-mild-
  hypercapnia is the anaesthetic target, and why an acetazolamide
  study and a breath-hold study are not interchangeable.

4.  THE CRYING CHILD: ONE PaCO2 STEP, OPPOSITE OUTCOMES
==============================================================================
  Hyperventilation to PaCO2 25 mmHg.  Two things happen at once:
  demand falls (protective) and the collateral conduits constrict
  (harmful).  Which dominates depends only on whether the arteriole
  has any range left to answer with.

                  normal       50.0       26.3   0.526      19.7        no
       preserved reserve       49.8       23.7   0.476      19.7        no

    adult, decompensated       19.3       15.7   0.811      19.7   already
    child, decompensated       18.7       14.9   0.798      19.7   already

  In the normal brain a 15 mmHg fall in PaCO2 costs 44% of the
  flow and is entirely safe, because the flow was never the
  constraint.  In the decompensated hemisphere it costs less in
  percentage terms and is an infarct, because there was no margin.

  JAM Trial (Miyamoto 2014, PMID 24668203): 5-y rebleeding 11.9%

  CONSERVATIVE rate was used to set HEM_HAZ0, so the HR is a

  a third (0.49 against 0.355).  Reported as a miss, not a fit: the
  most likely reason is that the model routes the whole benefit
  through pruning of the periventricular bed, whereas a real bypass
  probably also lowers the transmural pressure those vessels see
  faster than the bed can regress.
  Its mechanism in the model is visible in the Q_pva
  column: raising P_A collapses the gradient across the
  periventricular anastomosis, VEGF falls, the bed is pruned, and
  the wall stress that was going to rupture goes with it.  The
  operation does not reinforce the fragile vessel -- it retires it.

7.  HYPERPERFUSION IS FOCAL, RELATIVE, AND PRICED IN PRE-OP ISCHAEMIA
==============================================================================
  A LUMPED TERRITORY CANNOT HYPERPERFUSE: even a patent graft leaves
  the territorial arteriole still dilating, so mean territorial CBF
  never exceeds demand.  The syndrome therefore has to be FOCAL --
  the cortex the graft is sewn to, which sees P_F >> P_A -- and it
  has to be RELATIVE, measured against the flow that cortex had
  adapted to.  50 mL/100g/min is normal, and it injures a barrier
  that has lived on 18 for five years.

       pre-op hemisphere  REMOD  CVRi% CBF_F pre  CBF_F pk  rel pk  pk day  d>1.35  oedema  CBFws +
       preserved reserve  0.000   31.4      50.0      50.0    1.00   68.34     0.0   0.000     -0.6
       reserve just lost  0.593    0.0      38.1      65.7    1.41    7.67     9.0   0.166      3.2
       decompensated 2 y  0.593    0.0      37.1      65.2    1.43    7.76    10.7   0.226      4.4
       decompensated 5 y  0.593    0.0      35.6      64.6    1.46    8.08    13.4   0.332      5.0
      paediatric, severe  0.593    0.0      37.2      65.2    1.43    7.43    10.4   0.216      4.8

  A hemisphere with reserve does not hyperperfuse at all: its
  barrier was already adapted to 50.  The syndrome belongs to the
  arteriole's HISTORY, not to the surgeon.  REMOD (vasoparalysis)
  sets the HEIGHT of the surge; the barrier's re-adaptation time
  constant sets its DURATION.

  AND THE INCIDENCE IS SET BY SOMETHING THE SURGEON CANNOT SEE.
  Sweep the pial coupling g_leak between the peri-anastomotic
  cortex and the rest of the territory.  It trades the TERRITORIAL
  benefit against the FOCAL surge, in opposite directions:

   g_leak   Q_byp    P_A    P_F  P_F-P_A  CBFws +  rel surge  d>1.35  syndrome?
     0.25    65.0   28.1   65.4     37.3      2.0       1.70    19.9        YES

  A WELL-COUPLED cortex spreads the graft flow over the territory:
  large territorial gain, no surge.  A POORLY-COUPLED recipient
  keeps the pressure locally: small territorial gain, a big focal
  surge.  Since g_leak is a property of the patient's pial network
  and not of the operation, this is the model's account of why
  hyperperfusion occurs in a MINORITY of technically perfect
  bypasses, and why its occurrence is not a measure of surgical
  quality.  It also predicts the inverse correlation: the
  hemispheres that hyperperfuse are the ones that gained LEAST.

14.  SELECT BY PHYSIOLOGY, NOT BY ANGIOGRAPHY -- BUT WHICH?
==============================================================================
  FIRST, A NEGATIVE RESULT THAT CHANGES THE QUESTION.  Stratifying
  this population by INTRINSIC RESERVE at 5 y does not work:
    reserve quartile cut-points: 0.00% / 0.00%
    fraction of the cohort with reserve < 5%: 99.0%
  Reserve is a SATURATING variable.  It is exquisitely informative
  while it is being spent (section 2: it hits zero at year 2 while
  flow is still normal) and it carries almost no information once
  it is gone, because by then everyone is at zero.  A cross-section
  of established disease therefore cannot be stratified by it -- and
  that is a statement about WHEN to measure, not about whether the
  measurement matters.

  WHAT STILL VARIES AT 5 YEARS IS THE MARGIN TO THRESHOLD.
  margin = CBF_ws - CBF_crit:  P10 -7.1   median -0.3   P90 +12.8 mL/100g/min
  quartile cut-points: -3.75 / +5.11

  Offer the SAME direct bypass at year 5 to the worst- and best-
  margin quartile, and read year 8.

                   group   n  margin   STEN    gS  INF% none  INF% byp   gain  Pisch none  Pisch byp     ARR
       worst margin (Q1)  36   -7.21  0.870  0.82      26.44     18.62   7.82       0.546      0.409   0.137
        best margin (Q4)  36  +12.51  0.543  1.39       1.95      0.89   1.07       0.147      0.093   0.055

  The two groups differ in what the tissue is actually getting,
  and they differ by ~7x in what the operation is worth.  But read
  the STEN column honestly: the angiogram is NOT blind here (mean
  stenosis 0.870 vs 0.543), so in this population the lesion and the
  physiology are correlated and a DSA does carry real prognostic
  information.  The defensible claim is the weaker one: stenosis is
  informative but NOT SUFFICIENT, because the same stenosis maps to
  different margins depending on collateral conductance and on
  haemoglobin (sections 2, 10 and 12).

  Taken with the negative result above, the model's selection
  claim is narrower and more useful than 'measure reserve':
    - BEFORE decompensation, reserve is the sensitive variable and
      flow is uninformative (section 2, year 1-2).
    - AFTER decompensation, reserve is saturated and the margin to
      CBF_crit is the variable that still separates patients -- and
      because CBF_crit depends on haemoglobin, the same CBF means
      different things in different patients (section 10).
