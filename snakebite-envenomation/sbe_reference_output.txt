SNAKEBITE ENVENOMING + ANTIVENOM — QSP REFERENCE MODEL RUN
독사 교상 + 항독소 — QSP 참조 모델 실행 결과
==============================================================================

  50 ODEs · 4 toxin classes · 3 antivenom fragment types · 8 snake
  archetypes · 22 scenarios · 300-subject virtual population.

  THESIS:  antivenom binds; it does not undo.  Two clocks — a venom
  clock in hours and a substrate clock in days — and every endpoint
  is read off the slow one.

==============================================================================
A.  ANTIVENOM AND VENOM DISPOSITION — HALF-LIVES ARE COMPUTED, NOT ASSERTED
==============================================================================

  Antivenom products (2-compartment):
  product                             t1/2 alpha   t1/2 beta   mgNE/vial
  ovine Fab (CroFab-like)                  0.88h       21.8h         8.0
  equine F(ab')2 (ANAVIP-like)             2.65h      126.8h         8.0
  equine whole IgG (Indian polyvalent ASV-like)       3.25h      136.8h         6.0

  Venom toxin classes (two parallel depots + one systemic compartment).
  The TERMINAL half-life is flip-flop-limited by the slow depot, not by
  systemic elimination — the venom leaves the body faster than it leaves
  the bite.  That is the number an antivenom has to outlast.
  class      MW  f_slow   abs fast   abs SLOW   systemic   TERMINAL    eps
  SVMP       50    0.35      2.77h      57.8h      8.09h      44.7h   1.20
  SVSP       30    0.32      1.98h      46.2h      6.07h      36.5h   1.00
  PLA2       14    0.15      1.39h      23.1h      3.08h      19.3h   0.60
  TFTX        7    0.05      0.58h       6.9h      1.85h       6.0h   0.35

  >>> THE RECURRENCE INEQUALITY (claim 2), evaluated:
        venom SVMP terminal (absorption-limited) t1/2 =   44.7 h
        ovine Fab terminal t1/2       =   21.8 h   ratio 0.49  -> Fab LOSES the race
        equine F(ab')2 terminal t1/2  =  126.8 h   ratio 2.84  -> F(ab')2 wins it
        equine whole IgG terminal t1/2=  136.8 h   ratio 3.06  -> IgG wins it
      Recurrence is therefore a property of the FRAGMENT, not of the dose
      and not of the operator.

==============================================================================
B.  HOW MANY VIALS IS 'ENOUGH'?  — the labelled potency, done as arithmetic
==============================================================================

  mgNE required = sum over toxin classes of (mass in the bite / eps_class).
  Vial counts below are what the label's mgNE/vial delivers for a 1.0x bite.

  snake                          venom mg  mgNE need  IgG vials  Fab vials
  Daboia russelii (Sri Lanka)          63       61.0       10.2        7.6
  Echis ocellatus                      22       18.0        3.0        2.2
  Naja naja                            40       85.7       14.3       10.7
  Bungarus caeruleus                   10       17.1        2.8        2.1
  Crotalus atrox                       55       34.3        5.7        4.3
  Bothrops asper                       50       48.8        8.1        6.1
  Bitis arietans                       90       74.1       12.3        9.3

  Read the Daboia and Naja rows together.  Naja naja carries LESS venom
  protein than Daboia russelii (40 mg vs 63 mg) and yet needs MORE
  neutralising capacity (85.7 vs 61.0 mgNE), purely because 55% of its
  mass is three-finger toxin, the class antivenom covers worst
  (eps = 0.35).  The standard 10-vial Indian ASV dose is 60 mgNE: a
  0.98x margin against Daboia and a 0.70x margin against Naja.  The
  clinical habit of giving 20-30 vials for a neurotoxic cobra bite is
  not caution; it is stoichiometry.

D.  THE FOUR CLAIMS, EVALUATED AGAINST EACH OTHER
==============================================================================

  D1.  DEPTH vs SLOPE — antivenom moves the nadir, not the recovery rate

      regimen                     FG nadir  unclot h   rise @ FG 1.0  FG day 7  SCr peak
      none                            0.06      39.2    0.46 g/L/day      2.58      2.12
      10 vials @ 1 h                  2.40       0.0  n/a (never <1)      2.93      0.97
      10 vials @ 4 h                  0.58       0.0    0.82 g/L/day      3.24      1.31
      10 vials @ 12 h                 0.06      18.5    0.96 g/L/day      3.27      1.60
      20 vials @ 4 h                  0.61       0.0    0.81 g/L/day      3.24      1.30

      fibrinogen nadir spans 0.06 - 2.40 g/L   (42-fold)
      rise rate measured at the SAME fibrinogen level (1.0 g/L) spans 0.46 - 0.96 g/L/day
      = a 66 % spread in the slope against a 42-fold spread in the nadir.
      hepatic synthesis ceiling (k_syn x SYNUP_max) = 1.55 g/L/day — every observed
      rise rate above sits under it.
      The nadir is a function of when the antivenom arrived.  The rise rate
      is not: it is the liver's synthesis term, and neither the antivenom
      dose nor its timing appears anywhere in that term.  The residual
      2-fold spread in the slope is NOT the antivenom either — it is
      residual venom still consuming fibrinogen on the way up, which is
      why the FASTEST rise belongs to the 12-hour arm (deepest deficit,
      largest acute-phase SYNUP) and not to the earliest-treated one.

      Doubling the vial count at a fixed 4 h delay: FG nadir 0.58 -> 0.61 g/L. Timing is not a dose.
      Cryoprecipitate (substituting for the liver): P(bleed) 3.1 % -> 0.6 %.
      Tranexamic acid: FG nadir 0.576 -> 0.580 g/L, P(bleed) 3.1 % -> 3.1 %.
      TXA is unchanged to three significant figures because 95% of the
      fibrinogen loss is direct enzymatic cleavage, which plasmin
      inhibition cannot touch.

  D2.  RECURRENCE IS THE MOLAR MARGIN OVER THE TAIL, NOT THE VIAL COUNT

      Fab 6 v, 1x bite                                       1.40x     3.6x     124 h     1.7999      221
      F(ab')2 6 v, 1x bite                                   1.40x     <LOD      none     0.0003        0

      At an IDENTICAL molar dose and margin, swapping ovine Fab for equine

      recurs depends on the bite size they happened to receive.

      and permanent scar from 12.1 % to 5.1 %.

  D5.  WHERE ANTIVENOM CANNOT GO, AND WHAT CAN

      regimen                                             necrosis   CK peak   CP mmHg  venom AUC

      (free-venom AUC 13.10 mg.h/L) and still leaves 18.1 % myonecrosis,

      entire rationale of the pre-hospital small-molecule programme.

==============================================================================
E.  WHAT THIS MODEL DOES NOT REPRODUCE (and what would have to be true)
==============================================================================

  The Fab arm reproduces recurrent venom ANTIGENAEMIA (a 3.6-fold rebound
  peaking on day 5).  It does NOT produce severe recurrent
  HYPOFIBRINOGENAEMIA, which is also reported clinically after ovine Fab.
  The reason is arithmetic:

      hepatic fibrinogen synthesis ceiling         =   1.55 g/L/day
      peak late (>72 h) fibrinogen consumption     =  0.094 g/L/day  (6.0 % of it)
      shortfall factor needed for a second fall    =   16.5x

  ROUTE 1 — put more of the bite into the slow depot.  f_slow cannot exceed
  1.0 (the whole bite sequestered), so the available headroom is bounded:

      f_slow (SVMP)      late flux  % ceiling  recur antigen  recur coag
      0.35                   0.094       6.0 %      yes 124 h          no
      0.50                   0.261      16.8 %      yes 115 h          no
      0.70                   0.518      33.3 %      yes 105 h          no
      0.85                   0.707      45.6 %      yes 100 h          no
      1.00                   0.885      57.0 %       yes 97 h          no

  Even with the ENTIRE bite sequestered, the late flux stays far below the
  synthesis rate.  Depot release cannot be the mechanism of clinical
  recurrent coagulopathy.  That is a negative result, and it is the useful
  half of this section.

  ROUTE 2 — let the antivenom-toxin complex dissociate.  The complexed pool
  is large (tens of mg of toxin), so even a slow off-rate is a big source.
  This model's default is k_off = 0; sweeping it:

      k_off (1/h)     complex t1/2    late flux   % ceiling   recur coag   FG 2nd fall
      0.0000                   inf       0.094        6.0 %           no       0.12 g/L
      0.0020                 347 h       0.096        6.2 %           no       0.11 g/L
      0.0080                  87 h       0.105        6.8 %           no       0.08 g/L
      0.0200                  35 h       0.129        8.3 %           no       0.04 g/L
      0.0600                  12 h       0.214       13.8 %           no       0.04 g/L
      0.1500                   5 h       0.325       20.9 %           no       0.08 g/L

  Complex dissociation does not produce it either, at any swept rate —
  and the reason is instructive.  The complexed pool is cleared with an
  11.6 h half-life, so by 72 h only ~1 % of it is left.  Dissociation
  releases venom EARLY, while the pool is still large, which prolongs the
  acute phase instead of creating a late second event.

  THE CONCLUSION, STATED AS A NEGATIVE RESULT:

    Recurrent venom ANTIGENAEMIA after a short-lived fragment follows from
    absorption kinetics alone and needs no extra assumption.  A late second
    episode of SEVERE, unclottable-blood coagulopathy does not: it needs a
    late venom flux ~17x larger than a sequestered depot can supply,
    and neither a larger sequestered fraction (bounded at 57 % of the
    synthesis ceiling even if the WHOLE bite is sequestered) nor complex
    dissociation gets there.  Two readings survive, and they are
    distinguishable by measurement rather than by argument:

      (i)  the recurrent hypofibrinogenaemia reported after ovine Fab is
           mostly a LABORATORY event — a dip of a few tenths of a g/L, which
           is what this model does produce — rather than a return to
           unclottable blood; or
      (ii) there is a venom reservoir this model has no compartment for.

    This is reported rather than tuned away, because the alternative —
    raising f_slow to 1.0 and calling it calibration — would have buried a
    real discrepancy under a plausible-looking number.
