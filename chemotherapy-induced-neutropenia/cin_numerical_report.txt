==============================================================================
A1  Calibration — what is fitted, and the six places it misses
==============================================================================
TWO parameters are fitted, both to Vogel 2005, and they are hit exactly
because one parameter per number always is.  Everything else is fixed,
derived, or auxiliary.  The rest of this section is the list of reported
numbers the model then gets WRONG, which is the only part worth reading.

The reason there are only two fitted parameters is a trade-off the model
cannot escape.  The overall myelotoxic scale can be set EITHER so that
the absolute durations reported in the G-CSF trials come out right, OR so
that the model tells regimens apart.  Not both.  The duration below 0.5
SATURATES in this topology: the recovery limb starts from a floor set by
phi, which does not depend on exposure, and climbs at a rate set by the
feedback exponent, so once the proliferative pool is empty at all, extra
exposure buys almost no extra duration.  Raising the scale until CAE
reaches Crawford's 6-day median puts every combination regimen at a
nadir of 0.065-0.11 and a DSN of 5.4-6.8 d -- indistinguishable.  The
scale is therefore set for DISCRIMINATION and the shortfall reported.

Six misses.  They are not independent, and reading them together says
something more useful than any one of them:

  (1) CAE's unsupported duration is 23% short of Crawford's median.
      That is the discrimination trade-off above, taken deliberately.

  (2,3) THE MODEL EXAGGERATES HOW MUCH THE TIMING OF G-CSF MATTERS.
      Given on day 2, pegfilgrastim removes severe neutropenia
      completely (0.00 d against Green's observed 1.8).  Given on day 4,
      filgrastim leaves 1.90 d on CAE against Crawford's observed 1.0.
      One is -100% and the other +90%, in OPPOSITE directions, and
      the axis they differ on is when the drug was started.  So the
      timing sensitivity reported in A4 -- which is one of this model's
      more useful outputs -- is real in DIRECTION and too STEEP in
      magnitude.  A reader should take A4's ranking of start days and
      not its absolute day-by-day numbers.

  (4) The model also ranks the two PRODUCTS in the wrong order.  Green
      found pegfilgrastim marginally worse than daily filgrastim (1.8
      vs 1.6 d); the model has it better (0.00 vs 1.46 d).  The mechanism
      responsible is the one the model is proudest of -- target-mediated
      clearance keeps the long-acting product present for exactly as
      long as the ANC stays low (A7) -- so the same term that produces
      the self-titration result also produces this error.  Holmes 2002
      reported the two products within '< 1 day' of each other and the
      model's gap is 1.5 d, so it is outside that too.

  (5) The intact-marrow response is under-predicted by roughly half.
      This one is forced.  The steady-state ANC multiple in a Friberg
      loop is ((1-phi)(1+Emax_amp))^(1/gamma) with 1/gamma = 5.9, so a
      5-fold rise needs Emax_amp = 0.320 -- and at that value
      pegfilgrastim abolishes severe neutropenia entirely, which it
      demonstrably does not.  Emax_amp = 0.100 is a compromise between two
      observations the model cannot hold at the same time.

  (6) AC comes out at P(FN) = 0.198 where the guideline band for that
      regimen is 10-20 per cent, and TAC at 0.211 where the band is above
      20 per cent.  TAC lands correctly; AC lands at the top of its
      band rather than in the middle, which is the residue of the same
      saturation.  The model therefore cannot be used to ASSIGN a
      regimen to a guideline risk band -- only to compare within one.

What survives all of this is everything COMPARATIVE.  A4 through A14
change one thing at a time within a fixed regimen and patient, and the
quantity being compared -- timing, product, dose, cell-cycle
dependence, count versus function -- does not depend on the absolute
scale being right.  The absolute P(FN) values do, and they are anchored
to a randomised-trial population, which Truong 2016 shows understates
real-world rates.

A2  The two parameters that do not interact: WHEN and HOW DEEP
==============================================================================
The claim the whole model rests on is that the nadir DAY is a function
of the transit time and the nadir DEPTH is a function of the exposure,
and that they are separate.  Both halves are testable.

(a) DOSE SWEEP at fixed transit time.  I expected this table to show
    the dose moving the DEPTH.  It does not, and the model is right and
    my expectation was wrong: what a threefold dose range buys is
    DURATION.  Once the proliferative pool has been emptied, more drug
    cannot empty it further -- it can only keep it empty for longer.

tumour layer is a thin log-kill and is the weakest thing in this file,
so read the tumour column as a PRICE LIST, not as a survival estimate.

==============================================================================
A6  Same-day pegfilgrastim, and why the model says it is worse
==============================================================================
Giving the growth factor with the chemotherapy rather than the day
after is convenient and is known to be inferior.  In this model the
reason is structural rather than empirical: G-CSF raises the
proliferation rate, the cytotoxic kills in proportion to the
proliferation term, and if the two overlap the drug simply gets more
cells.  No parameter encodes 'same-day is bad'.

==============================================================================
A7  Pegfilgrastim clears itself: the drug is eliminated by the cells it makes
==============================================================================
PEGylation removes the renal route, so the only clearance left is
receptor-mediated internalisation by neutrophils and their precursors.
The consequence is that a single FIXED 6 mg dose is self-titrating: the
sicker the marrow, the longer the drug stays.

Same fixed 6 mg dose in every row.  The half-life measured during the
nadir (day 5-8) rises from 27 h in the mildest regimen to 92 h in the
most severe, a 3.4-fold range, while the half-life measured after
recovery (day 15-18) barely moves.  Reported pegfilgrastim half-lives
span roughly 15-80 h in exactly this way.

That is not between-patient variability and it is not a covariate: it
is the same feedback loop read from the drug's side.  A patient whose
marrow is in trouble automatically receives a longer exposure, because
the mechanism that would have cleared the drug is the mechanism that is
missing.  It is why pegfilgrastim is dosed as a flat 6 mg regardless of
weight while filgrastim is dosed per kilogram per day.

==============================================================================
A8  The ANC on G-CSF over-reads marrow output
==============================================================================
Five separate G-CSF actions are implemented.  Only two of them make new
cells; the other three move or preserve cells that already existed.
Switching them on one at a time, in an INTACT marrow, splits the
measured ANC rise between production and bookkeeping.

In the intact marrow the redistribution terms carry a large share of the
rise.  In the emptied marrow they carry almost none, because they are
bookkeeping operations on a pool that is empty -- the benefit there is
production and pipeline speed.  The clinical reading is that an ANC
measured on a growth factor is not a measurement of marrow output.
Note also which single action does most of the therapeutic work: it is
TRANSIT, not amplification -- the pipeline, not the factory.

==============================================================================
A9  What the answers depend on: phi, the stem layer, and the cumulative hit
==============================================================================
phi = 2^-n_div is the reciprocal of the mitotic amplification factor,
and it sets an ANC FLOOR that no cytotoxic can push through.  It is the
single most consequential structural choice in the model, so it is
swept rather than defended.

n_div      phi  predicted floor  nadir ANC  DSN (d)  P(FN)
-----  -------  ---------------  ---------  -------  -----
5      0.03125            0.236      0.440     1.44  0.016
6      0.01562            0.124      0.325     2.77  0.072
7      0.00781            0.066      0.236     3.80  0.163
8      0.00391            0.035      0.169     4.69  0.265
9      0.00195            0.018      0.119     5.33  0.354

Every column moves with n_div, which is what makes this the most
consequential single choice in the model.  n_div = 5 puts the floor at
0.236, above the deepest routinely observed nadirs, so the model then
cannot produce a grade 4 nadir at any exposure at all.  Raising n_div
lowers the floor, deepens the nadir and -- because the recovery limb
starts from that floor -- lengthens the duration: DSN goes from 1.44 d at
n_div = 5 to 5.33 d at 9.  The value used, 8, is the smallest one at
which the model reaches grade 4 nadirs on the regimens that produce
them clinically; going further buys duration at the cost of the
regimen discrimination discussed in A1.  Reported myeloid amplification
spans roughly 2^5 to 2^9, so 8 sits inside the range, but it should be
read as CHOSEN rather than measured.

The slow stem-cell layer has no direct human anchor.  Its only visible
consequence is CUMULATIVE deepening across cycles, so that is what is
reported, over six cycles of CAE:

A10  Trilaciclib: the protection is proportional to CELL-CYCLE DEPENDENCE
==============================================================================
Trilaciclib works by holding stem and progenitor cells in G1, so the
only part of the kill it can prevent is the part that requires the cell
to be cycling.  In the model that share is the agent property f_cyc,
and the prediction is a straight line through it: an S/G2M-specific
topoisomerase poison should be well protected and a cycle-nonspecific
alkylator or platinum agent should be barely protected at all.

I expected the DSN saved to rise monotonically with the cell-cycle
dependence column.  It does not, and the table is worth reading for the
reason.  Topotecan has the highest cycle dependence of any regimen here
and gains almost nothing, while GCb and EP -- lower on that column --
gain the most.  The missing variable is WHERE the regimen sits relative
to the 0.5 threshold.  Trilaciclib removes a FRACTION of the kill; on a
regimen whose nadir is far below the threshold, removing a fraction
still leaves it below, and the duration barely moves.  The benefit is
largest for regimens sitting NEAR the threshold, where a fractional
reduction in the kill is enough to lift the trough across it.

That is a sharper and more falsifiable claim than the one I started
with: the drug should help most where the neutropenia is severe enough
to matter and not so severe that partial protection is futile.  It also
means the licensed indication -- small-cell lung cancer on etoposide/
carboplatin -- sits in the model's favourable window, while relapsed
topotecan, where the drug is also used, does not.

Carboplatin and gemcitabine are platelet-limited in the clinic, and in
this table they are not.  The reason is traceable and worth stating
plainly: the per-agent potencies were set from nadir-depth RATIOS and
then multiplied by one global scale (sens_global = 2.00) chosen so that
the COMBINATION regimens discriminate.  A scale that is right for a
three-drug regimen is too large for a mild single agent, so every
monotherapy in the table comes out more neutropenic than it should be,
and the neutrophil column wins by construction.

Two things follow.  The model is usable for the COMBINATION regimens it
was calibrated on and for comparing INTERVENTIONS within a regimen,
which is what every other analysis here does.  It is not usable for
predicting single-agent toxicity grades, and a version that was would
need per-agent slopes fitted to per-agent data rather than to ratios.

A12  A clinician in the loop: what a course actually delivers
==============================================================================
The endpoint that matters over a course is not any single nadir, it is
the relative dose intensity that survives contact with the day-1 blood
count.  The controller below implements the rules that govern that:
hold a cycle for ANC < 1.5 or platelets < 100, reduce the dose after
two holds or after the cycle's FN risk exceeds 20%, and -- depending on
policy -- add pegfilgrastim as primary or secondary prophylaxis.

The FN trigger is the RISK crossing 20% rather than a simulated event,
because this is a deterministic model: an earlier version required a
per-cycle probability above 0.5, which never happened, so the
controller never fired and the whole analysis was vacuous.

chemotherapy.  Preserving dose intensity is not free; it is a decision
to spend one toxicity to buy another.

A13  Where does the 20% threshold come from?
==============================================================================
Guidelines recommend primary G-CSF prophylaxis when the regimen's FN
risk exceeds 20%.  The threshold used to be 40%, and what changed was
not the biology but the cost of an FN episode (Calhoun 2005; Cosler
2005).  The model can be asked what the BIOLOGICAL benefit looks like
across that range: if the threshold were biological there should be
something happening at 20%.

Baseline FN risk is varied by scaling the myelotoxic exposure, and at
each level pegfilgrastim is added:

The RISK RATIO is roughly flat across the whole range -- the growth
factor removes a similar FRACTION of the hazard wherever it starts --
while the ABSOLUTE reduction, and therefore the number needed to treat,
scales almost linearly with baseline risk.  There is no kink at 20%,
or anywhere else.  The model's answer is that the threshold is a
statement about the price of a hospital admission divided by the price
of a syringe, and nothing about neutrophils: it should move whenever
either price moves, which is exactly what happened between the 40% and
20% eras.

A14  The count and the defence can move in opposite directions
==============================================================================
Dexamethasone is given with every taxane as premedication and with most
regimens as an antiemetic.  It raises the ANC by demargination and
marrow release while lowering chemotaxis and the oxidative burst.  The
model carries FUNCTION as a separate state precisely so that this can
be shown rather than argued about.

premedication                 ANC @24h  marginated  function  effective  nadir   DSN   P(FN)  min function
----------------------------  --------  ----------  --------  ---------  -----  ----  ------  ------------
no dexamethasone                  4.87        4.90     0.872       8.52  0.234  4.01  0.1700         0.830
dexamethasone 8 mg BID x 3 d      5.09        1.99     0.729       5.17  0.243  3.97  0.1612         0.606

Dexamethasone raises the measured ANC at 24 h from 4.87 to 5.09 -- a 5%
increase, and exactly the kind of number that gets a patient through a
day-1 blood-count gate.  Over the same interval the EFFECTIVE defence
moves from 8.52 to 5.17, i.e. in the opposite direction, and P(FN) moves
from 0.1700 to 0.1612.

The same asymmetry, read through the infection module rather than
through the counts: bacterial load and temperature with and without
the steroid, on a regimen severe enough to produce an event.
intervention                  nadir   DSN  peak bacteria  peak CRP  peak temp   P(FN)
----------------------------  -----  ----  -------------  --------  ---------  ------
no dexamethasone              0.169  4.69         9877.7     229.8      39.76  0.2650

Levofloxacin does not change a single haematological number and still
moves P(FN), because it acts on the other side of the race.  A growth
factor changes every haematological number.  Reporting only the ANC
would make the antibiotic look inert and the steroid look protective,
and both readings would be wrong.
