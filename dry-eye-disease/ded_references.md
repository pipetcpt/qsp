# Dry Eye Disease (DED, 건성안) — Key References for the QSP Model

> Curated bibliography supporting the mechanistic map, mrgsolve ODE model, and
> Shiny dashboard. Each citation is linked to PubMed (PMID) where a PMID could
> be confirmed; DOI is given where the PMID could not be independently
> verified. Grouped by topic; an asterisk (★) marks **primary parameter
> anchors** for the model.

### Notes on parameter calibration

- Cyclosporine 0.05%/0.09%, lifitegrast, and loteprednol effect magnitudes and
  onset timescales are anchored to the Schirmer-responder rates and corneal/
  conjunctival staining changes reported in refs 19-25 (slow, weeks-to-months
  onset for CsA vs faster ~2-6 wk onset for lifitegrast and steroid).
- Perfluorohexyloctane and diquafosol are modeled as acting purely through the
  physical evaporation barrier (refs 26-27) and P2Y2 mucin/aqueous secretagogue
  pathway (ref 30) respectively, with **no direct anti-inflammatory arm**,
  consistent with their distinct, non-immunomodulatory mechanisms of action.
- Varenicline nasal spray is modeled via the nasal trigeminal-parasympathetic
  reflex arc (ref 28-29), not systemic absorption, consistent with its labeled
  mechanism.
- The self-perpetuating "vicious cycle" feedback (hyperosmolarity → inflammation
  → epithelial/goblet-cell damage → further instability) follows the TFOS DEWS
  II pathophysiology framework (refs 1, 5, 9) and the MMP-9/Th17 mechanistic
  literature (refs 12-14).
