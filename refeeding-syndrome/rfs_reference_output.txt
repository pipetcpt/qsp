TEST 2   the starvation phase - THE MODEL'S FIRST REAL TEST
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The claim being tested is that the pools empty while the BLOOD TESTS
STAY NORMAL.  If serum phosphate fell during starvation, admission
phosphate would be a useful triage test and refeeding syndrome would
not be dangerous.  The deficit here is SIMULATED, never asserted.

phenotype at day 60     BMI   FFM |     P     K    Mg    Ca |  ICF-P  ICF-K ICF-Mg |  thiam
                               kg | mmol/L                   |  %norm  %norm  %norm | %store
anorexia/starvation    17.9  41.1 |  1.02  3.60  0.72  1.15 |   85.4   75.6   81.1 |   18.5
alcohol use disorder   18.4  42.0 |  0.84  3.63  0.64  1.19 |   87.5   77.9   74.9 |    5.6
diuretic + cardiac     17.9  41.1 |  1.02  3.46  0.80  1.15 |   85.4   73.6   85.7 |   25.6
purging / GI losses    18.0  41.4 |  1.02  3.18  0.42  1.12 |   86.3   70.2   59.1 |   23.9

VERDICT: serum phosphate is 0.8-1.1 mmol/L in every phenotype - inside
   or at the edge of the reference interval - after two months of
   starvation.  Serum potassium and magnesium are only mildly low.
   Meanwhile the thiamine store is down to 5-25 % and the intracellular
   cation pools are down by 20-40 %.  The admission blood panel does
   not see the disease.  This is why NICE triages on HISTORY - body
   mass index, weight loss, days without intake, alcohol - and not on
   the laboratory result.

TEST 3   the flux arithmetic at the moment of refeeding
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   admission ECF phosphate pool          12.6 mmol
   day-1 demand  J_demand                25.7 mmol/d
   day-1 supply  J_supply                25.8 mmol/d
      of which absorbed from the feed    18.2 mmol/d
      of which net bone efflux            7.6 mmol/d
   day-1 Lambda_P = demand / supply      1.00

   urinary phosphate, admission         13.78 mmol/d
   urinary phosphate, day 1              0.84 mmol/d
   urinary phosphate, day 3              0.80 mmol/d

   Renal excretion is a THRESHOLD, GFR x max(0, Pser - TmP/GFR), so the
   kidney's entire contribution is to stop excreting.  That is worth
   13.8 mmol/d and it is SINGLE-USE: it is fully spent within a day,
   after which the kidney has nothing further to offer no matter how
   low the phosphate goes.  Against a demand of 26 mmol/d the whole
   renal reserve is worth about 13 hours.

RESULT 1   the 2x2: which prophylaxis is doing the work?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Full-rate feeding (30 kcal/kg/d from hour zero) in the same patient,
crossing electrolyte repletion with thiamine.

                            P nadir  lactate   mort % Wernicke %
neither                        0.45      5.0     1.07        4
electrolytes only              1.02      4.5     0.54        4
thiamine only                  0.45      3.9     0.51        0
both                           1.02      3.9     0.36        0

The two prophylaxes are NOT interchangeable and they do not overlap:
electrolytes fix the phosphate nadir and do nothing for Wernicke;
thiamine fixes Wernicke and the lactate and does NOTHING for the
phosphate.  Each closes a channel the other leaves open, which is why
giving one and not the other still leaves the patient exposed.

RESULT 3   the feed itself is a phosphate prescription
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three regimens at an IDENTICAL 20 kcal/kg/d and identical patient.

                                    P nadir      GIR  lactate   mort %
intravenous dextrose, 100 % CHO        0.07     3.47     10.5     5.75
enteral formula, 100 % CHO             0.19     3.47      6.9     3.73
enteral formula, 40 % CHO              1.00     1.39      4.0     0.64

Same calories, same patient, three different diseases.  A complete
enteral formula carries ~22 mmol phosphate, 38 mmol potassium, 6 mmol
magnesium and thiamine per 1000 kcal; a bag of intravenous dextrose
carries NONE of them unless somebody adds them.  Substituting the
route while holding the calories fixed moves the phosphate nadir from
0.07 to 0.19 mmol/L.  Dropping the carbohydrate fraction from 100 % to
40 % at the same energy moves it again, to 1.00, because the demand
tracks the GLUCOSE, not the calories.

RESULT 4   admission phosphate is nearly uninformative
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Six patients presented for refeeding.  Their starvation histories
differ; their ADMISSION PHOSPHATE is essentially the same number.
Every one is then fed on the identical unprophylaxed regimen.

history                      adm P     adm K   ICF-K %   P nadir   mort %
30 d at 50 % energy           1.03      4.08      82.7      0.68     0.41
45 d at 40 % energy           1.02      3.87      78.9      0.52     0.56
60 d at 32 % energy           1.02      3.60      75.6      0.45     1.07
75 d at 28 % energy           1.02      3.34      73.7      0.46     1.28
90 d at 25 % energy           1.02      3.08      72.4      0.51     1.29
120 d at 22 % energy          1.02      2.62      71.8      0.71     1.46

Admission phosphate spans 1.02-1.03 mmol/L across a FOUR-FOLD range of
starvation duration - a spread of 0.02 mmol/L, well inside assay noise
and entirely inside the reference interval - while modelled mortality
varies %.1f-fold over the same six patients.  The number on the
admission panel does not carry the information.  The history does.

UNEXPECTED, and reported because it runs against the obvious guess:
the phosphate nadir is NOT monotone in starvation duration.  It
deepens to 0.45 at 60-75 days and then becomes SHALLOWER again at 120
days (0.71).  The most wasted patients have the least lean mass and the
most insulin resistance, so they cannot mount the glycolytic flux that
generates the demand.  Their mortality still rises, because it is
driven by potassium, magnesium and thiamine rather than by phosphate.
A shallow nadir in a very wasted patient is therefore NOT reassurance;
it is the model predicting that serum phosphate is the wrong marker in
exactly the patients who are most at risk.

RESULT 6   phosphate repletion has an optimum, not a maximum
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Prophylactic intravenous phosphate is swept in a full-rate refeed.
Too little leaves Lambda_P above 1; too much exceeds the calcium-
phosphate solubility product and precipitates, taking the ionised
calcium down with it.

HONEST READING, including where the model FAILS to show what was
expected.  The benefit saturates: essentially all of it is bought by
the first 0.5 mmol/kg/d, which is the band the guidelines recommend and
which the model was never shown.  Beyond that the mortality curve is
FLAT to within the resolution of the hazard model.

The U-shape this section was written to demonstrate is REAL but it lies
far outside clinical practice.  The calcium-phosphate product only
crosses the 4.4 mmol^2/L^2 solubility limit at 12 mmol/kg/d - twenty
times the recommended dose - and only there does ionised calcium fall
and mortality turn back up.  At every dose a clinician would actually
write, the precipitation term never engages.

So the asymmetry inside this model is stark and one-sided: UNDER-
repletion costs 0.50 percentage points of mortality, while over-
repletion costs nothing measurable until the dose becomes absurd.  If
that is right it favours the ASPEN posture of repleting generously and
not withholding feed.  The caveat is that the model has no soft-tissue
calcification, no phosphate-infusion arrhythmia and fixed renal
function, all three of which are the real reasons clinicians cap the
rate - so the flat top of this curve should be read as 'this model
cannot see the harm', not as 'there is none'.

Two readings, and the first one contradicts the teaching:

(a) the famous 'thiamine BEFORE the glucose' rule buys nothing at the
    one-hour timescale on which it is usually taught - giving thiamine
    an hour early and giving it with the feed are indistinguishable
    here.  What the model does support is the WEAKER, and probably
    the real, version of the rule: thiamine must be given on DAY ZERO.
    Six hours late already costs; a day late costs more.

(b) phosphate is the opposite shape.  Starting it on day 0 abolishes
    the nadir; starting it 24 h later does not, because by then the
    cells have already taken what they needed out of a 13 mmol pool.
    Late phosphate still repairs the day-7 number, so it rescues the
    LABORATORY promptly and the PATIENT slowly - which is exactly the
    trap of treating this syndrome reactively.

RESULT 8   oral vs intravenous thiamine - A PREDICTION THAT FAILED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The intestinal thiamine carriers (ThTR-1/ThTR-2) saturate: the model's
maximum absorption rate is 7.5 umol/h (~2.0 mg/h) whatever dose is
swallowed.  Intravenous dosing has no such ceiling.

This section was written to show that oral thiamine cannot replete an
acutely deficient patient.  THE MODEL DOES NOT AGREE, and the
disagreement is reported rather than tuned away.

                             TK 3 h  TK 12 h  TK 24 h    TK d3     WE %
no thiamine                    0.01     0.02     0.04     0.26       10
oral 100 mg/d                  0.16     0.95     1.00     1.00        0
oral 300 mg/d                  0.23     0.97     1.00     1.00        0
oral 1500 mg/d                 0.28     0.98     1.00     1.00        0
IV 100 mg/d                    0.70     1.00     1.00     1.00        0
IV 500 mg/d                    1.00     1.00     1.00     1.00        0
oral 300, vomiting             0.01     0.04     0.14     0.69        5
IV 100, vomiting               0.70     1.00     1.00     1.00        0

The saturable carrier turns out not to be the binding constraint.  Its
ceiling is 7.5 umol/h = 48 mg/d of absorbed thiamine, and the daily
REQUIREMENT is only 1.4 mg.  Saturated absorption still delivers
roughly thirty times what the patient needs, so an intact gut repletes
the store within a day whatever the route.  Raising the oral dose from
300 to 1500 mg/d changes nothing, exactly as predicted - but so does
switching to the vein, which was NOT predicted.

The intravenous route earns its place on the last two rows instead: if
absorption is impaired - vomiting, ileus, an alcohol-damaged mucosa -
the oral arm collapses and the intravenous arm does not.  The model's
verdict is that 'IV because oral cannot deliver enough' is the wrong
reason, and 'IV because you cannot rely on this patient's gut' is the
right one.  Since that is precisely the population in question, the
clinical recommendation survives with its justification replaced.

RESULT 9   magnesium: how you give it, and what it unlocks
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(a) the same 0.4 mmol/kg/d as a 2 h bolus or as a 24 h infusion
      bolus      Mg day 3 0.72 mmol/L
      infusion   Mg day 3 0.88 mmol/L
    Renal magnesium handling is a THRESHOLD, so the peak of a bolus is
    excreted rather than retained.  The prescription chart shows the
    same daily dose; the patient does not receive it.

(b) potassium repletion with and without magnesium repletion
      K 2 mmol/kg/d alone         K day 3 3.77, Mg day 3 0.65
      K 2 mmol/kg/d + Mg          K day 3 3.86, Mg day 3 0.88
    Hypomagnesaemia releases the intracellular magnesium block on ROMK,
    so the distal nephron wastes potassium.  The effect is real in the
    model but MODEST at this repletion rate (3.77 vs 3.86 mmol/L on day
    3): a 2 mmol/kg/d potassium infusion is large enough to outrun the
    leak it is fighting.  The model therefore supports 'correct the
    magnesium' as good practice but does NOT reproduce the textbook
    picture of potassium that is refractory until magnesium is given -
    and that is a place where the model may be understating a real and
    frequently reported clinical phenomenon.
    Magnesium matters through a second, quieter route as well: it is
    required for PTH secretion, and PTH is what makes bone donate
    phosphate, so magnesium sits upstream of the phosphate supply line.

RESULT 10  the ventilatory cost of feeding on glucose alone
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                   RQ     VCO2     PaCO2    mort %
100 % carbohydrate               0.95    0.214      98.2      3.73
40 % carbohydrate                0.87    0.196      77.9      0.64

    Substituting fat for carbohydrate at constant energy lowers the
    respiratory quotient, but note the SIZE of the two terms before
    accepting the usual explanation: carbon dioxide production differs
    by only about a tenth between these arms, while the modelled PaCO2
    differs by a quarter.  Most of the gap is not the CO2 load at all -
    it is the ventilatory pump, because the same glucose load has
    driven the phosphate down and taken the diaphragm's energetics with
    it.  The carbohydrate is not mainly overloading the lungs; it is
    weakening them, and then loading them.  A prediction that follows:
    the ventilatory benefit of a lipid-based feed should be largest in
    exactly the patients who are phosphate-depleted, and small in those
    who are not.

LIMITATIONS - stated, not buried
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   The worst scenario (phosphate-free intravenous dextrose, no repletion
     at all) bottoms out near 0.07 mmol/L.  Real reported nadirs in that
     setting are 0.10-0.30.  The model has no rescue behaviour by a real
     clinician, and its only brakes are transport saturation and the
     glycolytic block, so it over-shoots at the extreme.
   Mortality is a hazard model with ONE fitted scale.  The split between
     arrhythmic, cardiac-failure and respiratory death is structural, not
     fitted, and should not be read as three separately validated numbers.
   There is no sepsis, no organ failure, no delirium, no re-feeding
     induced hepatic steatosis and no vitamin deficiency other than
     thiamine.  Real refeeding deaths often involve those.
   The starvation phase assumes a constant intake fraction.  Real
     histories are episodic, and a binge-restrict pattern would deplete
     thiamine faster than this model does.
   Bone is a single well-mixed compartment with a PTH-driven efflux.
     Osteopenia of chronic starvation is not represented, so the model
     probably OVERSTATES the skeletal buffer in the most chronic cases.
   Intracellular phosphate is one soft-tissue pool.  Erythrocyte 2,3-DPG,
     myocardium and diaphragm are read off serum phosphate rather than
     given their own compartments.
   The insulin submodel is a minimal model calibrated at two points.  It
     is adequate for the direction and rough size of the refeeding insulin
     rise, and is not a glucose-kinetics model.
   Renal function is fixed.  Acute kidney injury, which is common in
     these patients, would change every threshold in the model.
