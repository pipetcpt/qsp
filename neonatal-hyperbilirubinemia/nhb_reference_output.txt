      one.  That is why the ET arm's free-bilirubin trace falls
      further than its TSB trace does (see A7).

==============================================================================

      Phenobarbital moves CN-II and does nothing whatever in CN-I.
      That is not a rule in the model: induction enters as
      GENO * ont * (1 + Emax*C/(EC50+C)), and GENO = 0 annihilates
      the induction term along with the enzyme.  You cannot induce
      a gene product that does not exist.

      READ THE THREE COLUMNS ACROSS.
      [G]     geometry alone: the ceiling rises 1.89-fold from birth
              to 18 y.  So the surface-to-mass argument is real.
      [G+P]   add the fall in bilirubin production per kg (7.7 ->
              3.4 mg/kg/day, i.e. the literature 8.5 -> 3.8 ratio)
              and the ceiling goes FLAT (0.84-fold),
              because BSA/W falls by 0.42 over the same interval and
              production per kg falls by 3.80/8.50 = 0.45.  The two
              terms very nearly cancel.  Geometry plus physiology
              therefore predicts that phototherapy should keep
              working - which contradicts the clinical course.
      [G+P+O] restore the age-dependent optical accessibility of the
              skin and the loss of control comes back (1.72-fold).

      CONCLUSION.  The usual explanation for why phototherapy stops
      controlling Crigler-Najjar as the child grows - 'the surface-to-
      mass ratio falls' - is quantitatively insufficient in this model,
      because it is almost exactly cancelled by the concurrent fall in
      bilirubin production per kilogram.  What is left to explain the
      failure is the optical term (thicker, more pigmented skin: less
      of the pigment pool lies within the blue-light penetration
      depth) together with achievable exposure time.  That is
      actionable in a way the geometric story is not: it says defend
      irradiance and hours-per-day, and replace the missing enzyme
      before the optical term wins.

      HONEST CAVEAT ON THE ABSOLUTE NUMBERS.  This model runs the
      CN-I phototherapy ceiling at 4.9-8.4 mg/dL where the published
      clinical range is roughly 15-25 mg/dL.  The single well-mixed
      extravascular compartment is the reason: it lets the whole
      extravascular pigment pool refill the irradiated layer at one
      fast rate constant, so it overestimates how much of the body
      burden the photons can reach.  A deep, slowly-exchanging pool
      would raise the whole column.  The RATIO across the column -
      the 1.72-fold rise, and its cancellation in [G+P] - is a
      statement about scaling exponents and is insensitive to that
      offset.  Scaled onto a clinically observed neonatal ceiling of
      ~15 mg/dL, a 1.72-fold rise reaches ~26 mg/dL in adolescence,
      which is where transplantation or gene transfer becomes
      unavoidable.
